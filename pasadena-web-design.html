What usually goes wrong

A great business with a website that undersells it.

The most common thing I see in Pasadena isn’t a bad business. It’s a good one represented by a site built years ago, by someone who’s no longer reachable, that nobody has been able to update since. The hours are stale. The photos are from a different era of the business. Half the pages describe services that quietly ended.

That site is doing real damage, because it is the first impression for every person who hasn’t been referred to you. Fixing it is less about design taste and more about making the site tell the truth about what you are now.

And once it does, it has to keep telling the truth — which is why I stay on afterward instead of handing you a login and wishing you luck.

Questions I actually get asked

Straight answers.

I already have a website. Can it be fixed, or does it need replacing?

I'll tell you honestly after looking at it. Sometimes the bones are fine and it needs content and search work. Sometimes it's built on something no longer worth maintaining and replacing it is cheaper than fighting it. Either answer is free to get.

Nobody can update my current site. Is that normal?

Unfortunately, yes, and it's the single most common reason people call me. Whatever I build, you own, and changes are a quick ask rather than a project you have to chase somebody down for.

Do you work with Pasadena businesses if I'm not downtown?

Yes. Pasadena, Altadena, South Pasadena, San Marino, Sierra Madre. I'm in Burbank, which is a straightforward drive for a real meeting.

How do you approach SEO?

It's part of the build, not a separate invoice. I start from what your customers are actually typing, make sure the site answers those searches clearly, and keep your Google listing consistent with it. No guaranteed rankings, because those aren't a thing anyone can honestly promise.
