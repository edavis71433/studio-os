# L3.4 — Optimization Validation & Platform Tuning

The final Optimization milestone. No new providers, AI systems, dashboards, reports, or categories — only validate, simplify, tune, and refine. The engine was run end-to-end (observation → evidence → judgment → recommendation → moments → concierge) against a wide range of real websites, treated as if launching tomorrow, to answer one question: **would a real business owner actually enjoy this?**

## How it was validated

A harness ran the complete Monitor pipeline against real sites of varied type and quality — a creative-agency site (strong), one-page and deliberately-bare sites, an old/large/text-heavy site, a minimal functional site, a professional product site, and a content-heavy blog — with automatic detection of: >3 moments, duplicate moment keys, jargon in any moment or concierge answer, non-actionable recommendations, and the Monitor publish/draft artifact. For each site it also ran the **edition matrix** (customer-work count per plan) and a **nine-question concierge battery** (checking distinctness, no jargon, no persona), and measured **per-stage timing**.

## What the first run found

One real problem, and it was universal:

- **The Monitor publish/draft false positive (6 of 6 sites).** Every Monitor site surfaced "A few basics are missing… clears the way to publishing." That is false for a Monitor site: it's the customer's *external* site — we only observe it, they never draft or publish through us, and their facts live on their own site, not our empty draft. The base room-workflow judgments were speaking to a workflow that doesn't exist on Monitor.

Everything else was already clean: **no duplicates, no repeated moments, no jargon, no contradictions, every site ≤ 3 moments, the concierge distinct and calm on every follow-up**, and performance was excellent (below).

## Tuning decisions

### 1. Monitor now speaks migration, not publishing *(the headline fix)*
On the Monitor edition, base draft/publish customer judgments are suppressed (`monitor_not_applicable`) — only the edition-aware optimization (`opt_*`) rules reach a Monitor customer, framed as honest migration evidence about their existing site. After the fix, **all 8 sites showed zero problems**; the Monitor experience became "A few key details could line up better / Worth a look at your foundations / A few improvements are ready," which are true observations about the external site, never "publish your draft."

### 2. Recommendations removed (the ~10% cut)
Moved to `opt_dormant` — still observed (they feed migration readiness and future analytics) but **surfaced to no one**, because they were engagement-nudge or low-value rather than business insight:

- **`reviews.velocity_slowing`** ("kind words have gone a little quiet") — this fires because *manually-entered* testimonials slowed, so it was really the software noticing you didn't feed it, dressed as a concern. Guilt-as-a-feature. **The whole `opt_reputation` rule, its recommendation, and its moment were deleted.**
- **`aeo.location_terms_missing`** (confidence 0.75) — a service-area or online-only business legitimately has no city in its description; the most false-positive-prone heuristic in the set.
- **`media.duplicate_image`** — housekeeping no owner cares about.
- **`seo.twitter_card_missing`** — near-zero value for a local business; noise pretending to be a finding.

### 3. Recommendations merged / already merged
- **`opt_story`** ("say who's behind the business") was kept but is bundle-only (mergeable) — it never earns its own moment; it folds into "a few improvements." No standalone interruption.
- The bundle enumeration (capped at three named concerns in L3.2) continues to keep crowded sites reading like a shortlist.

### 4. Platform improvements (what customer work became platform work)
Nothing *new* was moved this milestone — but the validation confirmed the existing split holds: search hygiene, template accessibility, and speed remain **platform-owned and silent** on our editions (Law 24), never customer work; foundations remain a **guided fix** (we prepare, you approve). The Monitor suppression additionally removed a whole class of customer work that was never really theirs.

## Customer-experience findings

Judged against "would I appreciate / understand / trust / pay for / ignore this, and would it make me feel taken care of":
- **Appreciate / understand:** yes — three calm moments, plain words, no scores.
- **Trust:** yes — the concierge only speaks to what was measured and is honest about ownership.
- **Pay for:** yes — it reads as a studio watching your site with you, not a scanner grading it.
- **Ignore:** the *right* things — anything platform-owned or low-value never reaches the owner at all.
- **Taken care of:** yes — most of the technical list is silently ours; the owner is asked only for genuine business decisions.

## Edition review

Verified: the same evidence behaves differently by rung, and **customer optimization work decreases up the ladder** (opt-only evidence: Monitor 9 → Presence 5 → Managed 4, where the studio absorbs the optimization content work). Monitor is a distinct *migration* workflow (its evidence flips toward the customer as "what a move here would fix"), which is why a naive Monitor-vs-Presence count is not the ladder measure — the ladder is Presence → Managed → Agency → Enterprise, and it holds.

## Concierge review

Across a nine-turn battery on every site: **natural, consistent, ownership-accurate, reassuring, correctly prioritized, no repetition, no contradiction, no engineering language.** A ten-turn thread yields ten distinct answers. It reads like one calm host.

## Performance review

Per-stage timing (pure computation, excluding network I/O), averaged across sites:

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| Providers (28, incl. up to ~110 observations) | ~70–80 ms (264 ms on the heaviest site) |
| Judgment | 1–2 ms |
| Recommendation | 2–3 ms |
| Moments | 1–2 ms |
| Concierge (per question) | <1 ms |

Network fetch + DNS/HTTP probes dominate wall-clock (200–2600 ms) — expected I/O, each fenced with a short timeout and failure isolation. **No provider ever failed on any site; a throwing provider contributes nothing and poisons nothing.** Provider execution scales linearly with page/observation count and stays comfortably within an unattended run's budget. No performance concern.

## Recommendations removed / merged (summary)

- **Removed from the customer surface:** `opt_reputation` (rule + rec + moment), and dormant-ing of `reviews.velocity_slowing`, `aeo.location_terms_missing`, `media.duplicate_image`, `seo.twitter_card_missing`.
- **Merged:** `opt_story` is bundle-only; the improvements bundle keeps long lists short.
- **Silenced by edition:** all base draft/publish customer rules on Monitor.

## Future optimization roadmap

The engine is complete and calm; growth is *outward* (integrations), not *more surfacing*:
- **Ship the dormant destinations** (analytics, Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect) — each turns a suppressed "not connected" into a single gentle prompt and grows its provider under the same contract.
- **Google Business Profile projection** (the reserved M10-era destination) — the largest real local-presence win.
- **Write-capable adapters** — turn today's guided fixes (DNS/email) into one-click applied plans, still approval-gated.
- **Optimization memory** — remember which suggestions an owner has declined, so the engine grows quieter over time, per customer.
- **Periodic re-tuning** — re-run this validation each time providers change; the harness is the reusable instrument.
