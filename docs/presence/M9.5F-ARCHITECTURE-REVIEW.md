# M9.5F — AI Creative Studio: Architecture Review & Consolidation

*Review only. No code was written, no schema changed, no deployment made. Every recommendation here is a recommendation, not an implementation.*

---

## 1. Architecture Review

The AI Creative Studio (M9.5A–E) sits on top of a perception pipeline (M9.0–9.4). The milestone brief draws it as one long chain; **that is not the real shape, and the real shape is better**:

```
PERCEPTION (always running, observation → language)
  Evidence → Judgment → Recommendation → Business Moments → Concierge
                                                      ↓
                                              THE CUSTOMER DECIDES
                                                      ↑
STUDIO (invoked by the customer, parallel tools — not a pipeline)
  Writer (create) · Editor (improve) · Reviewer (critique) · Guardian (protect) · Coach (plan)
                                                      ↓
                        ONE GATE: Draft Writer → Draft → Preview → Publish (human ritual)
```

Writer→Editor→Reviewer→Guardian→Coach is not a flow a document passes through; they are five tools on one bench, each entered by the customer, all converging on the same single gate (the Draft Writer, unchanged since M7) and the same human approval. This diamond — perception in, decision in the middle, one write gate out — is the platform's strongest structural property. Nothing in M9.5A–E violated it: the Reviewer, Guardian, and Coach modules have **no write path to content at all** (asserted by tests that read the source), and the Writer/Editor produce proposals that only the customer can apply.

**Structural health, verified in code and tests:**
- Every subsystem has a deterministic tier that works with AI off (manual parity holds).
- Every model output passes a sanitizer that kills fake locations, unquotable evidence, invented categories, and ungrounded ideas.
- Every constant that matters (`approval_required`, `manual_possible`) is a CHECK constraint, not a convention.
- Every handoff is a fixed table the model cannot choose from.
- Law 13 holds: internal numbers exist (`internal_metrics`, confidence), and none reach a customer.

The architecture is coherent. The problems found below are problems of **accumulation** — five tools built one at a time, each correct alone, now showing seams where they meet.

## 2. Responsibility Matrix

| Subsystem | Its job (one sentence) | NOT its job | Could another do it? | Would a customer understand the difference? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Writer** | Create new content from the customer's facts. | Improving existing text; judging anything. | No — unique. | **Yes** — "draft it with help." |
| **AI Editor** | Improve existing content in place, facts preserved. | Creating from nothing; deciding what needs improving. | No — unique. | **Yes** — "improve it with help." Before/after makes it self-evident. |
| **AI Reviewer** | Critique writing quality; findings only. | Fixing anything; brand identity. | Partially — see Overlap #1. | **Mostly** — "a look over your writing" reads clearly. |
| **Brand Guardian** | Protect identity consistency; findings only. | Judging quality; touching content. | Partially — see Overlap #1. | **No, not reliably** — a first-time owner cannot say why a "tone" note lives in the brand desk instead of the review desk. |
| **Growth Coach** | Surface timed, grounded opportunities; prepare, never execute. | Ambient urgency (Moments' job); executing anything. | Partially — see Overlap #2. | **Yes** — "what's worth planning for" is plainly different from writing/reviewing. |

The Writer/Editor pair is the model the rest should aspire to: they share one drafts table, one accept path, one desk UI, one Fact Guard — merged plumbing, distinct verbs. The Reviewer/Guardian pair shares almost nothing despite doing structurally identical work.

## 3. Overlap Analysis

**Overlap #1 — Reviewer vs Guardian (the significant one).**
The Reviewer's 21 categories include `brand_voice`, `tone`, and `consistency` (verified in `reviewer/rules.ts:16-22`). The Guardian's 16 categories include `tone_drift`, `personality`, `terminology`, `capitalization`, `professionalism`. These are the same customer concern split across two desks, two near-identical 15-field contracts, two tables, two route families, two report renderers, and two ✦ buttons ("Look over my website's writing" / "Does it all sound like me?"). A customer who runs both gets two overlapping reports with separate dismissal memories — dismissing a tone note in one desk does not quiet the same observation in the other. The engines are cheap; the split *surface* is the cost.

**Overlap #2 — Coach vs Business Moments.**
Both surface "things worth doing." Moments: ambient, max three, judgment-driven. Coach: on-demand, up to eight, calendar/pack-driven — but five of its sixteen areas (`content_freshness`, `review_request`, `trust_building`, `faq_opportunity`, `homepage_refresh`) are re-derived directly from the same Evidence Engine observations that already flow through Judgment → Recommendation → Moments. The same quiet-updates evidence can appear as a Business Moment *and* a Coach opportunity, worded differently, with independent dismissal memories. A customer who dismissed the moment will meet its twin in the coach desk and reasonably conclude the product doesn't listen.

**Overlap #3 — `presence_voice` vs Brand Profile (data model).**
`presence_voice` (tone_notes, preferred_vocabulary, never_claim — M9.5A) and `presence_brand_profile` (personality, voice_characteristics, preferred_vocabulary, words_avoid, never_claims + 11 more — M9.5D) both claim to be where the customer's voice lives. The Writer/Editor read the former; the Guardian reads the latter. A customer who updates "words to avoid" in the brand profile has not changed what the Writer avoids. This is a quiet correctness bug waiting to be noticed, not just untidiness.

**Overlap #4 — duplicated internal machinery (maintainers only).**
`reviewer/rules.ts` and `guardian/rules.ts` each independently define: a finding contract, a severity sort, a sequence-counter factory, a sanitizer with the same kill rules, and a fixed handoff table. ~80% structurally identical. Category *names* also collide across layers (evidence has `trust`/`freshness`/`local_presence`; the reviewer has `trust`/`freshness`/`local_presence` meaning subtly different things). Customers never see this; every future maintainer will.

**Not overlapping (verified):** Writer/Editor boundaries are clean. Concierge remains the only conversational surface. Nothing in the studio feeds back into Evidence — there are no perception loops, and that absence is a feature (recommend naming it a law: **studio output is never evidence**).

## 4. Value Review

Test: removes meaningful work / solves a real business problem / a consultant would charge for it / customers would miss it / AI because valuable, not because possible.

| Capability | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Writer | **Keep** | Blank-page work is the #1 reason small-business sites stay empty. Consultants charge real money for exactly this. |
| Editor | **Keep** | "Make this read better without changing my facts" is a copyeditor's invoice. Compare-view earns trust. |
| Reviewer | **Keep** | A copy audit is a deliverable agencies sell. Zero-findings-on-clean-content keeps it honest. |
| Guardian engine | **Keep** | Consistency drift is real and invisible to owners; the avoid-list check alone (customer's own law, enforced) is distinctive. |
| Guardian's 16-field Brand Profile | **Trim** | Mission + vision + elevator pitch + brand promise + 12 more is consultant-brief homework. Most owners will complete 3 fields. The three that drive deterministic checks (words to avoid, personality, reading level) deliver ~80% of the value. Recommend: surface those three; collapse the rest behind "more about your brand" (UI change only — the schema is fine). |
| Coach calendar/seasonal/promotion | **Keep** | "It's three weeks before Mother's Day and you're a restaurant" is precisely what marketing retainers bill for. |
| Coach evidence-gap areas | **Move** (see Overlap #2) | Value is real but it's Moments' value, re-earned in a second surface. |
| Coach `gbp_post` area | **Watch** | Weakest grounding (inferred from absence of disconnection evidence). If it misfires in practice, fold it into the holiday opportunity as a next-step line rather than a separate card. |
| Model idea tier (Coach) | **Keep, barely** | Survived only because the grounding-quote rule kills generic output. If sanitizer kill-rates run high in production, deterministic-only is acceptable. |

**Nothing was found that exists only because AI can do it.** The value filter (M9.5E) and the zero-findings-is-a-fine-answer stance (M9.5C/D) are doing their jobs.

## 5. Workflow Review

The Draft → Preview → Publish tail is correct and untouched. Two documented improvements (not implemented):

1. **Cross-surface dismissal respect.** When the Coach generates an evidence-grounded opportunity, it should check active Moments (and their dismissals) covering the same evidence fingerprint and stay silent. One suppression query; no schema change.
2. **Handoff round-trips.** Reviewer → "Fix with help" → Editor works, but after accepting the edit, the originating finding stays open until the next review run. A finding whose target was edited since the report could display "you've worked on this since." Cosmetic, low priority.
3. **Codify the loop-free law.** "Studio outputs never become Evidence" is currently true by accident of construction. Write it into constitution 05 so a future milestone can't innocently add a reviewer-findings evidence provider and create a feedback loop.

## 6. Customer Journey Review

A first-time owner sees verbs, not subsystem names — the UI never says "Reviewer" or "Guardian" (good; keep forever). But the business tab now stacks **four ✦ buttons** (`polish / look over my writing / does it all sound like me / what's worth planning for`) and the room holds **13 ✦ marks** total. Five doors into AI from one screen is too many decisions.

Judgments, without documentation:
- *Draft it with help / Improve it with help* — instantly clear. ✅
- *A look over my writing* vs *Does it all sound like me?* — the customer cannot predict which door answers "is my About page okay?" ❌
- *What's worth planning for?* — clear. ✅
- The Concierge — clear, and underused as a router (One Host Law says it should be THE front door to all of this).

**Recommended simplification (UI only):** collapse the four stacked buttons into one — "**✦ Look things over**" — opening a single sheet with three plain choices: *my writing* (Reviewer + Guardian, one combined report), *what's coming up* (Coach), *polish it all* (Editor site-pass). Per-item ✦ buttons stay where they are; they're contextual and don't compete.

## 7. Simplification Recommendations (ranked)

1. **One review surface.** Merge Reviewer + Guardian *presentation*: one desk, one combined report, findings grouped as "how it reads" / "whether it sounds like you," one dismissal memory. Engines can stay separate modules initially; retire the Reviewer's `brand_voice`/`tone`/`consistency` categories in favor of the Guardian's (they're better grounded — they read the Brand Profile).
2. **One voice source.** Converge `presence_voice` into the Brand Profile: Writer/Editor read the profile's fields (falling back to `presence_voice` during transition). Fixes the quiet split-brain in Overlap #3.
3. **Coach yields to Moments** on evidence-gap areas (cross-suppression, Workflow #1).
4. **One shared findings substrate** (internal): a `findings/` module owning the contract, factory, sanitizer core, and severity sort; Reviewer and Guardian keep only their categories and detectors. Do this the next time either file is touched, not before.
5. **Progressive Brand Profile** (three fields first).
6. **Reuse fresh reports.** All three desks regenerate (and call the model) on every open. Serve the existing open report if it's younger than ~24h with a visible "look again" action. Cost and latency both drop; behavior gets calmer.

## 8. Roadmap Recommendations

| Planned | Recommendation | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Memory** | **Dissolve — do not build as a subsystem.** | Memory already exists where it's used: Guardian `learned`, Coach `(opp_key, opp_hash)` memory, Moments dismissal TTL, Concierge threads. A central "AI Memory" would be a subsystem in search of a job and a privacy surface in search of an incident. If anything, build a small internal *read* API ("what has this customer accepted/dismissed/preferred") consolidating what exists. |
| **Visual Intelligence** | **Merge into Guardian + Evidence.** | The Guardian already reviews photography/assets; Evidence already has a media category. Deeper visual checks are new detectors in existing homes, not a sixth studio tool. A first-time owner should never meet a "Visual Intelligence." |
| **Video Intelligence** | **Remove from the roadmap.** | Presence has no video content type, no video surface, no video evidence. Intelligence about content that cannot exist on the platform fails the value filter definitionally. Revisit only if/when video becomes a content type. |
| **Document Intelligence** | **Reshape as a Writer input.** | The valuable version is "hand us your old menu/price sheet/PDF and the fact sheet fills itself" — an *ingestion* capability strengthening the Fact Law, not a studio member. As a standalone analyzer it would be AI-because-possible. |

Net: the studio should stay at **five members indefinitely**. Growth goes into depth (detectors, packs, verticals), not headcount.

## 9. Product Review — does it still feel like a presence platform?

Mostly yes, with early warning signs. What keeps it a presence platform: verbs instead of product names; sentences instead of scores; zero-findings honesty; the manual path everywhere; AI marked by one quiet glyph (✦) rather than branding. What threatens it: the four-button stack (a visible AI shelf rather than help inside work), and desks that *generate on open* (making AI an event rather than ambient help).

Recommended stance, written down for future milestones: **the AI succeeds when the customer describes outcomes, not tools** — "my site stays current, sounds like me, and reminds me before Mother's Day" — and no feature should ever require the customer to learn a subsystem's name. The consolidations in §7 are also the answer here: fewer doors, calmer defaults, AI dissolving into the workflow it serves.

## 10. Commercial Review

- **Strategy fit:** every studio member maps to something a consultant/agency invoices (copywriting, editing, audits, brand guidelines, marketing calendar). The AI story supports the Managed Presence → Agency editions naturally: the same engines, more human review on top. ✅
- **Differentiation risk:** entitlement today is binary (full/readonly). All editions get the whole studio. Recommend (later, product decision): Monitor = perception only; Presence = Writer/Editor; higher editions = Reviewer/Guardian/Coach. The clean module boundaries make this a router-level gate whenever wanted — no rearchitecture needed. That optionality is worth protecting.
- **Operating cost:** all model calls are Haiku-tier with small prompts and deterministic fallbacks — bounded. The one avoidable burn is regenerate-on-open (§7.6). Per-customer worst case stays trivially cheap; at fleet scale the 24h-reuse rule is still worth it. No capability creates runaway cost; nothing calls a model in a loop.

## 11. Things To Remove / Merge / Protect

**Remove**
- Reviewer categories `brand_voice`, `tone`, `consistency` (cede to Guardian) — after the surfaces merge, not before.
- Video Intelligence from the roadmap.
- Nothing else. No subsystem earns removal.

**Merge**
- Reviewer + Guardian *surfaces* (one desk, one report, one memory).
- `presence_voice` → Brand Profile (one voice source).
- AI Memory (roadmap) → the memories that already exist.
- Visual Intelligence (roadmap) → Guardian detectors + Evidence providers.
- Document Intelligence (roadmap) → Writer fact-sheet ingestion.
- Internally: one shared findings substrate for Reviewer/Guardian.

**Protect (the ten-year assets)**
- The **Draft Writer single gate** — every write path converges on one audited door.
- The **Fact Guard** and the fact-sheet-as-only-truth law.
- **Deterministic tiers everywhere** — the product works with AI off; models are replaceable tenants, not load-bearing walls.
- **Schema-enforced constants** (`approval_required=true` as CHECK constraints).
- **Sanitizers between every model and every store** — the model never gets to lie.
- **Law 13** — sentences, never scores.
- **Fixed handoff tables** — the model never chooses where things go.
- **The loop-free property** — studio output is never evidence (codify it).
- **Verb-named UI** — customers never learn subsystem names.

## Final Questions, answered honestly

**Is the AI architecture still coherent?** Yes. The diamond (perception → decision ← studio, one gate out) is sound, and every subsystem honors it. The incoherence risk is at the surface layer — five doors where a customer needs two — not in the structure.

**Is every AI subsystem valuable?** Yes, all five pass the consultant test. Two carry trimmable fat: the Guardian's 16-field intake and the Coach's evidence-gap areas that re-earn Moments' value.

**Should any subsystem be merged?** At the surface: Reviewer + Guardian, yes — one "look it over" experience. At the engine layer: not yet; merge the presentation first, extract the shared findings substrate opportunistically, and keep the engines separable until the merged surface has lived a while.

**Should any subsystem be removed?** No. From the *roadmap*: Video Intelligence yes, and AI Memory / Visual Intelligence / Document Intelligence should stop being future subsystems and become properties of existing ones.

**Does the AI remain invisible until it provides value?** Imperfectly. Per-item help and the Concierge are invisible-until-useful; the four-button stack is a visible AI shelf, and generate-on-open makes AI an event. Both have cheap fixes (§6, §7.6).

**Does Studio OS still feel like a business platform rather than an AI platform?** Yes, today — because of verbs, sentences, and manual parity. The trend needs guarding: this review is the right moment to consolidate, because the next tool added without consolidation would tip the room from "help where you work" to "a drawer of AI products."

**What would you change if this had to last ten years?** Three things. (1) Treat the *contracts* — evidence item, finding, opportunity, draft snapshot — as the real product and freeze them the way the constitutions are frozen; engines, models, and UIs will all be replaced, contracts shouldn't be. (2) Do the voice-source merge soon; split-brain data models are the kind of debt that compounds silently. (3) Adopt the rule that growth means depth (detectors, packs, verticals) not breadth (new studio members) — five tools a customer can name by verb is a durable product; nine is a control panel.
