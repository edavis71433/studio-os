# L3.2 — Optimization Recommendation Experience

L3.1 taught Studio OS *what* optimization matters. L3.2 is *how* it helps — the experience. No new providers, evidence, observations, scores, dashboards, or reports. The whole milestone is a felt quality: optimization recommendations should feel like thoughtful business guidance, never like an SEO audit.

## The recommendation philosophy

Every optimization recommendation, when a customer engages with it, answers five questions — in plain business language, on demand, never all at once:

1. **Why does this matter?** — in terms of customers, trust, being found, or turning a visit into a booking; never in terms of tags or records.
2. **Why now?** — usually "worth a look soon — not an emergency." Urgency is reserved for the genuinely urgent.
3. **What happens if I ignore it?** — "Nothing breaks, and nobody will chase you." It stays on the list without nagging.
4. **Can Studio OS handle it?** — for platform-owned work, yes and silently (Law 24); for guided fixes, "we prepare it, you approve."
5. **Do I need to decide anything?** — only when a decision is truly the customer's to make.

These are answered by the **Concierge**, on the customer's terms, not pushed. The moment itself is a calm one-liner; the depth is there if they ask for it.

## Grouping strategy (unchanged principle, verified experience)

Many observations become one moment, grouped by the customer's job-to-be-done (L3.1), and mergeable improvement concerns fold into a single "a few improvements are ready when you are." L3.2 capped that bundle's enumeration at three named concerns plus "a few more small things," so a crowded site reads like a friend's shortlist, not an inventory. The **three-moment law is absolute**: a real site with ~34 raw observations shows exactly three calm moments.

## The recommendation lifecycle

1. **Observed** — evidence emitted (silent; the customer never sees this).
2. **Judged** — grouped into a business task; audience chosen by ownership + edition (L3.1).
3. **Recommended** — the full contract attached (evidence, business reason, expected benefit, effort, undoability, a manual alternative always available, approval required). Implementation detail stays internal.
4. **Surfaced** — only if it resolves to a customer audience and clears the noise floor; at most three moments.
5. **Explained** — the Concierge answers the five questions in plain words when asked.
6. **Resolved** — the customer acts (or the platform does, silently). On the next observation the evidence is gone, the judgment finds nothing to match, and the moment quietly disappears. **Re-observation confirms completion; nothing is ever marked "done" by hand.**
7. **Dismissed** — stays gone unless the evidence changes, worsens, or the dismissal ages out (30 days).

## Guided Fix behavior

For work Studio OS can do but that changes infrastructure (domain, DNS, email authentication, verification records — the `opt_foundations` task), the experience is **prepare-then-approve**: the platform prepares the exact change as a plain-language plan; the customer only approves; nothing to their web address, email, or security happens until they say yes (the frozen M12/M14 infrastructure-approval law). The Concierge frames it exactly this way:

> *"This is one Studio OS sets up for you: we prepare the exact change as a plan you can read in plain words, and you just approve it. Nothing to your web address, email, or security happens until you say yes."*

The customer is never handed a raw record to paste, never shown a DNS/DMARC term. The work is ours; the consent is theirs.

## Automatic platform improvements (Law 24)

Search hygiene, template accessibility, and speed (`opt_search_hygiene`, `opt_technical_access`, `opt_speed`) are platform-owned. On our editions they resolve to `audience: none` — recorded, suppressed, **never customer work**. They are corrected once at the template/renderer/hosting layer and confirmed by re-observation (the evidence stops firing). If a customer asks the Concierge about one, the honest answer is ownership, not a task:

> *"This is one of ours to handle — on a website built and hosted here, it's taken care of for you, so there's nothing on your list for it."*

Verified: platform-owned judgments are suppressed on Presence/Managed and appear as customer-facing migration evidence only on Monitor (where we don't yet own the site).

## Edition behavior (customer work decreases up the ladder)

| Edition | Optimization experience |
|---|---|
| **Monitor** | The most is shown — but framed as honest migration value ("bringing your site here would set this right"), because it's the customer's own external site. |
| **Presence** | Only genuine business decisions and knowledge reach the customer; platform work is silent; foundations are guided fixes. |
| **Managed** | Even the optimization content work moves to the studio (concierge); only true business knowledge (their own facts) reaches the customer. |
| **Agency / Enterprise** | Optimization work sits with the agency/brand team; the client is interrupted least. |

Verified in code (L3.1 ladder test): customer interruptions decrease Monitor → Presence → Managed.

## Customer-experience guidelines (for future optimization copy)

- **Lead with the customer's world**, not the mechanism. "People can't picture the place," not "no images."
- **Name the job, not the finding.** One moment per job-to-be-done.
- **Calm by default.** No urgency unless genuinely urgent; no exclamation marks; "nothing is wrong" framing for opportunities.
- **No jargon, ever.** The tone lint forbids SEO/schema/DNS/CDN/DMARC/WCAG/score/grade/%. If a term needs explaining, it doesn't belong in customer copy.
- **No scores, grades, percentages, or counts** presented as a verdict. Health is a sentence.
- **Own what's ours.** If the platform can fix it, say so — or say nothing. Never hand the customer our homework.
- **Guide, don't dump.** Infrastructure work is prepare-then-approve, never a record to paste.
- **The Concierge is opt-in depth.** The moment is calm and short; the five answers are there for whoever asks.

## Real-world validation

The complete experience (real external fetch → providers → judge → recommend → moments → Concierge Q&A) was run against a strong site, a minimal site, and a deliberately-bare one. Each showed exactly **three calm moments**, and the Concierge answered "why does this matter / how do I handle this / what if I ignore it" in plain business language with the guided-fix and platform-handled framings intact — no checklist, nothing overwhelming, no jargon. It reads like trusted advice.
