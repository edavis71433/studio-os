# Phase Q — Legal, Privacy, Compliance & Trust Foundation

*Closes FD-M3 — the last flagged pre-activation engineering item. Every published website now carries a generated legal foundation, produced from structured business facts through the one render pipeline. Consolidates the Legal Foundation / Compliance / Trust / Generated-Page-Architecture reports.*

## What shipped (verified by tests)

- **`lib/legal_pages.ts`** — one source, both templates render through their own shells:
  - **Privacy Policy (`/privacy/`)** — facts-true and plain: who we are (name + contact from identity/location), what the site collects (the contact form's name/contact/message, "used for one thing: getting back to you" — or, with no form endpoint, honestly "collects nothing you type"), hosting logs, your choices (see/correct/delete). **Effective date = the snapshot date** — deterministic, the only clock a renderer may read.
  - **Accessibility Statement (`/accessibility/`)** — promises the template *actually keeps* (headings/keyboard, written descriptions on every photo, contrast, reduced-motion, honest links) + "tell us and we'll fix it."
  - **Footer links** (Privacy · Accessibility) on every page of both templates; both pages in the sitemap and manifests.
- **The honest differentiator (verified before claiming):** both templates emit **no cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party embeds** (zero-JS business-classic; restaurant's only script reads a local JSON island; maps are links). So **no consent banner is required** — and the privacy page says exactly that. Competitors bolt consent managers onto tracking they added; Studio OS's correctness-by-construction means there's nothing to consent to.

## Competitor posture (Step 2)

Webflow/Wix/Squarespace/Shopify/WordPress: legal pages are the *owner's problem* (blank templates or paid plugins), and their sites ship trackers that then require consent tooling. AEM: enterprise legal is the customer's legal team. **Studio OS generates the foundation and keeps it true automatically** — when facts change, the next publish regenerates.

## Decisions (Steps 3–5)

- **Always exist:** Privacy + Accessibility (shipped). **Cookie Policy:** not needed as a page — the no-cookies fact lives in the privacy policy (truthful, simpler). **Terms of Service:** deliberately **not auto-generated** — terms make business-specific legal promises (refunds, liability, jurisdiction) a platform must not invent. **FD-Q1 (V1.1):** guided intake → drafted terms → owner approves → rendered like the rest.
- **Editable sections:** V1 generates entirely from facts (changing facts changes the page); custom legal additions ride FD-Q1.
- **Trust surfaces (Step 4):** name/address/phone/email/hours ✅ (footer + contact + schema); licensing/insurance/certifications/guarantees/review-highlights = the FD-T14 trust blocks (V1.1) — right home, already queued.
- **Consent banner (Step 5):** rejected for V1 as *unnecessary by construction*; becomes required only if embeds/analytics ever ship (noted in FD-T catalog items: adding a tracking-bearing block must bring consent with it).
