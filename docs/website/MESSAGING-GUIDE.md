# Davis Digital Studio — Messaging Guide

**2026-07-10.** Binding on all public website copy. Every page, every headline, every button, every alt text follows this. Pair with `WEBSITE-MASTER-PLAN.md`. **The Platform Secrecy Rule (§14) overrides everything — when in doubt, obey it.**

---

## 1. Voice
Davis Digital Studio speaks as **one person: Eric.** First-person singular ("I design…", "I stay on…"), never corporate "we/our team." The voice is that of a **trusted, senior expert who runs a calm, personal practice** — someone who under-promises, over-delivers, tells you the truth even when it costs the sale, and stays after launch. Confident because he's good, not because he's loud. The reader should feel *"this is a real person I can trust with my business,"* not *"this is a company selling me something."*

## 2. Tone
- **Calm.** No urgency tactics, no exclamation-mark energy. The premium signal is composure.
- **Warm, not casual.** Friendly and human; not slangy or jokey.
- **Confident, not boastful.** State capability plainly; let proof do the bragging.
- **Honest, sometimes disarmingly so.** ("What's shown will always be less than what's true." "Whether or not that means hiring me.") This candor is a differentiator — keep it.
- **Reassuring.** The subtext of every page is *"working with me feels organized and effortless; nothing slips."*

## 3. Writing style
- Short, clear sentences. One idea each. Read it aloud — if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
- **Lead with the client's outcome, then explain.** ("Get found by the people already searching" before "here's the SEO work.")
- Concrete over abstract. Specific over generic. Show a feeling, don't label it as "great service."
- Active voice, present tense. Contractions are fine (they're human).
- Parallel structure for power ("A website built to work. A partner built to stay.").
- Prefer plain words to industry terms. If a technical word is unavoidable, immediately say why it matters to them.
- No filler, no hedging stacks, no "we're passionate about." Cut every sentence that doesn't earn its place.

## 4. Vocabulary (use these)
- **What we sell:** websites, web design, SEO, getting found, ongoing care, a partner who stays, results, a calm/organized experience.
- **Offer names (canonical — use exactly these, everywhere):**
  - **Web design** (the build)
  - **SEO strategy** (getting found)
  - **Growth Partnership** (the ongoing relationship — *never* "Ongoing care", "ongoing partnership", "monthly retainer", or "maintenance")
  - **Services** (the hub — *never* "Solutions" as the page title/label)
- **The relationship:** "you work directly with me," "from the first call onward," "I stay on," "one person, paying attention."
- **The experience/portal (benefit words):** "one organized place," **"your project workspace"** (the canonical name for the client space — use this exact phrase everywhere; *never* "your private space," "client portal," or "dashboard"), "always know where things stand," "track progress," "approve work," "see invoices," "find your files," "get reports in plain English," "just ask."
- **Proof words (once earned):** the real credential, the real result, the real testimonial. Specific numbers only when true and permissioned.

## 5. Words to avoid
- **Hype:** "amazing," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "world-class," "game-changer," "next-level," "unleash," "supercharge," "!" as punctuation-by-default.
- **Corporate filler:** "we're passionate about," "solutions" (as a page name), "leverage," "synergy," "best-in-class," "seamless" (overused), "robust."
- **Fear-selling / pressure:** "you're losing customers every day," countdowns, fake scarcity. (Honest scarcity — "I take on a few clients at a time" — is fine because it's true.)
- **Anything that implies buying software** — see §14. Especially: platform, software, subscription, license, dashboard-you-get, tool-you-buy, "the system running this studio."
- **Fabricated proof:** invented metrics, stock "testimonials," logos you don't have the right to show.

## 6. Promises to make (and keep)
- **Personal delivery:** "You work directly with me — not an account manager." "You'll hear back from me, personally."
- **A response standard:** "I reply personally, usually within one business day."
- **Strategy first:** "I start with your business and your customers, not colors."
- **You stay in control:** "You approve every decision. Nothing goes live without your say."
- **Ownership & no lock-in:** the client owns their site; they can leave without penalty. ("Cancel anytime" on the Growth Partnership.)
- **Honesty:** "I'll tell you honestly what I think — whether or not that means hiring me."
- **Fee credit:** "Every audit fee is credited toward a project — you never pay twice."
- **A real number, on a call:** "I'll give you a real number for your business on the call." We do **not** publish prices — see §11.
- **After launch:** "I don't disappear after launch. When you need a change, you just ask."

## 7. Promises NOT to make
- **No guaranteed rankings or revenue.** SEO/ROI claims stay hedged and honest ("widely cited industry figures, not a claim about any specific result"; "estimates, not guarantees").
- **No specific numbers you can't back** (no "500 clients," no "3x traffic" without a real, permissioned case).
- **No instant/overnight results.** Premium work takes the time it takes.
- **No "cheapest" positioning.** We compete on outcomes and relationship, never price.
- **Nothing that implies the client is buying or licensing software.**
- **No claims of a large team, awards, or a portfolio we don't have.** Restraint is the brand.

## 8. How to describe the Client Portal
- **Frame:** a benefit of working with Eric — *"one organized place to see everything."* Describe **what the client can do and feel**, never how it's built.
- **Say:** "Track your project. Approve work. See invoices. Find your files. Get reports in plain English. Request changes — you just ask. Everything in one calm, private space, included with every project."
- **Never say:** "our platform," "our software," "CMS," "portal product," "login system we built," "dashboard you get access to as a subscription," or explain the technology.
- **Prefer:** **"your project workspace"** (canonical) / "one organized place" over the label "Client Portal" as a product name. Do not use "your private space" — superseded on 2026-07-10 by "your project workspace" for a stronger, more descriptive noun.
- Show it with tasteful, real screenshots framed as *"this is what you see when you log in."*

## 9. How to describe AI
- **Frame:** effortlessness and speed for the client — an outcome, never a mechanism or a feature you sell.
- **Say (as a benefit):** "Need a change? You just ask, and updates happen fast." "Content help when you want it, so your site never goes stale."
- **On the free tools:** it's fine to say the free review is "AI-assisted" (it's a public lead tool), described plainly — "a fast, honest read on your site."
- **Never:** "our AI platform," "AI content engine," "powered by [model]," "AI features/credits," or anything that sounds like a software capability the client licenses. Never explain how it works.

## 10. How to describe website management / editing
- **Frame:** "you don't have to touch anything — I handle it," or "changes are a quick ask, not a project."
- **Say:** "Manage your website without the busywork — tell me what you need changed and it's done." "No re-learning software, no wrestling with a builder."
- **Never:** "website builder," "CMS," "edit it yourself in the dashboard," "content management system," "self-serve editor." The client experiences *ease and being taken care of*, not *software they operate*.

## 11. How to describe ongoing support (Growth Partnership)
- **Frame:** momentum and a partner who stays — not "maintenance" (which sounds like a cost/chore).
- **Say:** "This isn't maintenance. It's momentum." "The same person, paying attention, every month." "Your site keeps up with you instead of slowly falling behind."
- **Include:** no lock-in ("cancel anytime") and the human ("it's still me").
- **Pricing:** **do not publish an amount.** Superseded on 2026-08-25 — the site published "starts around $1,500" / "$400 a month" / audit tiers, and now publishes nothing. Every number is quoted on the free call. `/pricing` explains **how** work is priced and what changes the number; it never says how much. Pinned by `tests/e2e/marketing.spec.ts` (MS2), which fails the build on any `$` amount on a money page.
- **Never:** "subscription," "SaaS," "license," "plan tier," "platform fee." It's a **service relationship**, priced monthly.

## 12. Headlines
- Outcome-led, parallel, ownable. Model on the keepers: *"A website built to work. A partner built to stay."* · *"The best customer is the one already searching."* · *"This isn't maintenance. It's momentum."*
- Avoid templated/generic H1s ("Web design for [industry]") — lead with the win ("Fill more tables. Get found by hungry locals.").
- One H1 per page; it states the client's gain.

## 13. CTAs
- Primary, everywhere: **"Book a free call →"** (one verb, one style). It's the site's single conversion goal.
- Benefit-led secondary CTAs on tools: "Get a free read on what's costing you clicks →." Never generic "Submit" / "Click here."
- One label per destination (no "Free tools" vs "Free site review" ambiguity); one phrase → one page.
- Reassure at the button: "I reply personally, usually within one business day."

## 14. 🔒 PLATFORM SECRECY RULE (absolute — overrides all of the above)
Until Davis Digital Studio is fully migrated onto the platform and operating successfully in production, **the public website must NEVER mention, name, imply, or hint at:**

> Studio OS · CMS · website builder · SaaS · platform · licensing · white-label · agency edition · platform/subscription-software pricing · "selling software" · an API · a product a customer could buy or license.

**Rules:**
1. **Customers buy Davis Digital Studio *services*, not software.** Nothing may imply otherwise.
2. **The platform exists entirely behind the scenes.** Every platform capability is described **only** as a benefit the client receives while working with DDS.
3. **Explain WHY it benefits the client, never HOW the technology works.** No architecture, no product names, no "our system."
4. **No public page may link to** `pricing.html`, `signup.html`, `help.html` (it links to pricing), or `studio-os-demo.html` — these are the platform surface and stay `noindex` + unlinked from marketing.
5. **Known soft spots to fix and never repeat:** "I built a proper client portal" (→ "every project gets its own private space"); the "Client Portal ↗" chip (→ a benefit label); services.html "the same capability behind the systems running this studio" (→ pure custom-build-for-the-client, no reference to our own systems); start.html budget option "Custom platform" (→ "Custom web application").
6. **When in doubt, cut it.** If a sentence could make the reader ask "wait, what software do they run?" — rewrite it as a client outcome or remove it.

**Litmus test for any new copy:** *Would a prospective client reading this think they are hiring a person to deliver a result, or buying/renting a piece of software?* It must always be the former.
