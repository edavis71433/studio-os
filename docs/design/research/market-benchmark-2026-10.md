# Market leaders converge on calm, one-click workflows

As of October 2026 the leading CRMs, website builders, client-portal tools and design systems have converged on a small set of copyable patterns, and the convergence is tighter than the category boundaries suggest. On the CRM side every product uses a three-zone record page (identity header, stage bar, merged activity timeline with a side column of related records), a Kanban pipeline whose hygiene comes from next-activity sorting and per-stage rotting timers rather than AI, address-matched email that lands on the record automatically, and a home screen that is a list of what needs you today. On the builder side the model is autosave plus a separate Publish that doubles as a pre-flight, a domain screen that ends in a green "Connected" chip with automatic SSL, and an AI-first onboarding that produces an editable site brief before generating anything. The client-facing tools agree on one emailed link where the client chooses, signs and pays in forced order without creating an account, a no-password per-project portal with a one-time emailed code only at the moment of signing or paying, and Stripe's hosted invoice page as the floor for payment UX. The cross-cutting layer is equally settled: AI that drafts but never ships without an Apply click, a ⌘K palette plus single-key creates, a 3–5 step setup checklist on Home, a two-tier notification inbox ("needs me" versus everything else), search-first help with an explicit human hand-off, Stripe's Customer Portal for billing, and a light-first, one-accent, 14px/8px-grid visual language with Atlassian's 50–600ms motion ladder. The measurable bar is modest and vendor-reported: **19.2% average checklist completion and 37.5% activation** ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/saas-product-metrics/)), **median time-to-value about 1.5 days** ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/time-to-value)), **87% of Stripe invoices paid within 24 hours** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/invoices)), **~80% of DocuSign agreements completed within a day** ([DocuSign](https://www.docusign.com/blog/top-5-e-signature-features-to-get-agreements-signed-faster)), **4.5:1 text contrast** ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)) and **toasts of about three words** ([Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/deprecated/toast)). The gaps the leaders leave open — first-class deliverable approval, internal task depth for tiny teams, Squarespace's missing autosave and version history, portals that are enabled silently — are where a small CRM + portal + builder can exceed the norm rather than merely match it.

## Record pages, pipelines and inboxes share one anatomy across all four CRMs

### Record page: three zones, a stored or computed "next step", and inline edit on hover

**Converged pattern.** Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive and Attio all build the contact, company or deal page the same way: a header strip with identity, a few key fields and action buttons; a stage or lifecycle bar directly beneath it on pipeline objects; a chronological activity timeline that merges emails, calls, meetings, notes and tasks; and related records in a side column. Salesforce's Lightning page puts a Highlights panel on top, Path beneath it with up to five Key Fields and 1,000 characters of Guidance for Success per step, and an activity timeline on the right under the Email / Log a Call / New Task composer ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lex_migration_whatsnew/lex_migration_whatsnew_crm); [Trailhead Path](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/visualize-success-with-path-and-kanban)). HubSpot's default layout is a header with **up to five admin-chosen properties, two quick activity buttons plus a "+"**, an Actions menu (Summarize, Merge, Clone, Follow), main-workspace tabs for Overview, Activities, Revenue and Intelligence, and a right sidebar of Key information, Associations and Attachments; inline editing is hover → pencil → edit in a pop-up → click outside to save ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-the-default-record-layout)). Pipedrive's deal view leads with a progress bar that shows the current stage and **how many days each completed stage took**, a left Summary sidebar whose sections can be dragged and toggled, and a timeline split into a collapsible **Focus** section (activities, email drafts and pinned notes needing attention) above a filterable History with a Changelog tab ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/deal-detail-view)). Attio's page shows up to six Highlights widgets, a Record Details section defaulting to the first five attributes, and tabs for Overview, Activity, Notes, Tasks, Emails and Files, all configured per object and applied workspace-wide ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/records/configure-record-pages)).

**Notable variants.** The products differ on where "what to do next" lives. HubSpot stores it as a deal property named "Next step" and layers a Breeze summary card on top, generated on load and regenerable but not customisable ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/summarize-records)). Pipedrive makes the next *activity* the organising principle and lets admins require an Outcome, Priority or Note before an activity can be marked done ([Pipedrive updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates)). Salesforce uses human-authored per-stage guidance plus a "Mark Status as Complete" button and optional confetti on Closed Won ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/visualize-success-with-path-and-kanban)). Attio relies on tasks in the timeline and computed attributes such as Last interaction, which count only participants, not linked records ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/records/add-record-activities)). HubSpot's email default shares full bodies; Attio's default shares subject lines and metadata only and hides bodies ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/email-calendar/sharing-emails.md)).

**The numbers.** Header fields: 5 (HubSpot), 6 highlights (Attio), 5 record details by default (Attio). Path: up to 5 key fields and 1,000 characters of guidance per step, keep paths to 20 or fewer stages, hovering shows time-in-stage ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lex_migration_whatsnew/lex_migration_whatsnew_crm)). Association cards show up to six properties and five quick filters ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-the-default-record-layout)).

### Pipeline: Kanban by default, hygiene by sorting and rotting, gating by required fields

**Converged pattern.** Kanban is the default pipeline view everywhere except Salesforce, where the list view is primary and Kanban is a display option chosen from the Displays menu, with column sums of Amount by default and drag-to-stage ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/visualize-success-with-path-and-kanban)). Cards carry title, contact, value, label and owner, and the record opens on click. Pipedrive's pipeline is the most complete hygiene loop that needs no AI: **deals are sorted by next activity by default**, an activity icon on each card lets you mark done or schedule the next one, per-stage "Rotting in (days)" turns tiles **red** after N inactive days, and marking an activity done automatically prompts you to schedule the next one ([Pipedrive pipeline view](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pipeline-view); [Pipedrive rotting](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature); [Pipedrive follow-up pop-up](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-can-i-disable-the-follow-up-activity-pop-up)).

**Notable variants.** HubSpot has the strongest gating: conditional stage properties can be marked Required so "users cannot create or update the record until they set a value", surfacing as a modal when a deal is created in or moved into that stage, and Pipeline Rules (Pro/Enterprise) restrict skipping stages or moving backward ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines); [HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/HubSpot-Ideas/Require-properties-before-moving-a-Deal-s-Stage/idi-p/369300)). Salesforce's Kanban shows a **yellow triangle** for overdue tasks, no open activities, or no activity for 30 days, and its Pipeline Inspection view highlights changes to amounts, stages and close dates, with a Summer '26 Activity column showing a rolling 30-day engagement heatmap ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lex_migration_whatsnew/lex_migration_whatsnew_crm); [Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/sales-cloud-top-7-salesforce-summer-26-features/)). Pipedrive's Forecast view is a third button that re-columns the Kanban by expected close month with projected revenue in each header ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/article/the-forecast-view-revenue-projection)). Deleted Pipedrive deals are restorable for 30 days ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pipeline-view)).

**The numbers.** Rotting: per-stage day count, timer runs from last update and is reset by completed activities, notes, files and email actions, but not by a scheduled future activity ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature)). Salesforce warning threshold: 30 days without activity. Won/Lost stages are required under Deal probability for HubSpot reports to work ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines)).

### Inbox and email: address matching puts every reply on the record

**Converged pattern.** "Reply lands on the record" is solved by address matching in all four. HubSpot's Conversations inbox gathers email, chat, Messenger, forms, WhatsApp and calling, logs forwarded mail to the sender's contact record by default, and routes via an "Automatically assign conversations" toggle to an AI customer agent, specific users or the contact owner ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/inbox/set-up-the-conversations-inbox)). Pipedrive's Sales Inbox uses **smart linking** to attach conversations to deals, leads and projects based on the addresses involved, shows per-email open (eye) and click (cursor) tracking, and offers Shared vs Private visibility ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/email-sync)). Attio's sync auto-creates People and Company records from your mailbox, which doubles as zero-effort import ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/syncing-people-and-companies)). Salesforce's Einstein Activity Capture now syncs emails as native Task and EmailMessage records, and unmatched addresses get an "Add as Contact / Add as Lead" affordance ([Salesforce Ben](https://salesforceben.com/sales-cloud-top-salesforce-spring-26-features)).

**Notable variants.** Attio's sequences are the closest fit for a one-person agency: a canvas with Wait blocks (minimum one day), a delivery window with business-days-only, exit on reply (always on) or meeting booked, AI variables that generate per-recipient text without consuming credits, an unremovable unsubscribe link, and sends routed through the user's own Gmail or Microsoft mailbox via an Outbox ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/automations/sequences/create-a-sequence)). Pipedrive rate-limits email actions to 40 per minute per company and auto-logs sent emails as completed activities since March 2026 ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/email-sync); [Pipedrive updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates)). HubSpot keeps the basic inbox on all plans but gates routing to a paid seat and multiple inboxes to Pro ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/inbox/set-up-the-conversations-inbox)). Among the client-portal tools, HoneyBook routes new client email to an Inbox separate from the notifications bell ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2459619-about-the-notifications-center-in-honeybook)), and Dubsado 3.0 added a Messages workspace with Inbox/Sent/Archive, scheduled send and canned templates ([Dubsado](https://dubsado.com/blog/introducing-dubsado-three-point-o)).

**The numbers.** Attio sequence limits: five minutes between emails from one mailbox, 12 per hour, 200 per day; enrol up to 1,000 people at a time ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/reference/automations/sequences/create-a-sequence)). Pipedrive: 40 email actions per minute per company ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/email-sync)).

### Home / "needs me": tasks due, today's meetings, hot deals, money in four buckets

**Converged pattern.** The common denominator is tasks due today or overdue, today's meetings, a short list of hot deals and unanswered leads. Salesforce Home is an admin-composed page of Performance, Assistant, Today's Tasks, Today's Events and Key Deals components ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lightning_app_builder/lightning_app_builder_homepage)). HubSpot's Sales Workspace, default since April 27, 2026, is a Summary of drag-reorderable cards (Tasks with a "Start tasks" button, Suggested tasks, Follow up on meetings, Outreach activities) plus Schedule, Feed and Insights ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sales-workspace/manage-sales-activities-in-the-updated-sales-workspace)). HoneyBook's homepage is the most prescriptive small-business model: AI chat first, then an overview bar (new leads, unread messages, tasks, YTD bookings), Leads, Tasks including **automation tasks awaiting approval**, Calendar, Create shortcuts, and a Payments overview split into **Paid / Processing / Upcoming / Overdue** coloured green, orange, gray and red, every section toggleable and reorderable ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5339081-about-your-honeybook-homepage)). Shopify Home shows daily tasks, order next-steps with counts, four configurable 30-day metrics (sessions, sales, orders, conversion), a feed of insights and an Alerts feed ([Shopify Home](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/shopify-home)); Wix Home opens with an "Ask me anything" box, a plan/domain/business-email status strip with Connect/Upgrade actions, and top stats ([Wix dashboard](https://support.wix.com/en/article/about-your-wix-dashboard)).

**Notable variants.** HubSpot's legacy workspace is the only product with an enumerable rules engine for "what needs me": guided actions fire when creation criteria are met and expire on conditions, with types such as newly assigned leads, email replies, contacts awaiting your reply, follow up on your meeting, log call outcome, update next steps and upcoming meeting preparation, each rateable "Yes, keep it coming!" or "Not really" ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/use-the-sales-workspace)). Pipedrive deliberately has no home: the Activities list with a contextual side panel (J/K to move between activities) and the next-activity-sorted pipeline are the home ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/activity-contextual-view)). Attio's Home is upcoming meetings, tasks and Ask Attio, with a separate Notifications inbox ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/getting-started/navigating-your-workspace)). Moxie's Homebase has three tabs, Focus / Calendar / Insights ([Moxie](https://www.withmoxie.com/product)). HoneyBook delivers an AI priority list through chat at **8:00 am local**, built from meetings, due tasks, payments and clients awaiting replies, and skips the day when nothing is found ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/11273338-use-honeybook-ai-s-priority-lists-to-kickstart-your-day)).

**The numbers.** HubSpot follow-up opportunities persist seven days or until finished ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sales-workspace/manage-sales-activities-in-the-updated-sales-workspace)); Shopify's metric window defaults to 30 days with four tiles ([Shopify Home](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/shopify-home)); HoneyBook shows the last three automations pending approval ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5339081-about-your-honeybook-homepage)).

## Automation, AI and keyboard access have settled on "draft, then Apply"

### Automation and AI: trigger → steps with a human approval gate; AI metered and logged

**Converged pattern.** Every CRM and client tool ships a trigger → condition → action builder, and the two UI shapes the market has settled on are linear (HoneyBook: one Trigger, then sequential Action / Wait / Condition steps, with an Activity tab for runs and manual cancel) and node-based (Dubsado Flows: each action has its own When, relative or fixed, with triggers grouped as "after this flow starts", "after all previous actions are completed", client progress, project dates and appointments) ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9493889-how-to-create-an-automation); [Dubsado help](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/15668762-flow-triggers-general)). HubSpot adds four trigger families (filter, event, schedule, webhook), a template library, undo/redo over 30 days and 90-day action logs ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows)). The gate that reviewers miss when absent is a human approval step: Dubsado's "Require approval before completing this action", HoneyBook's "automation tasks awaiting approval" on Home, and the Bonsai complaint that invoices went out without approval ([Dubsado help](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/3186297-workflow-actions-in-2-0); [HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5339081-about-your-honeybook-homepage); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/238825/Bonsai/reviews/)). The AI surface area that is now table stakes is a summary card on the record, thread summarise and draft reply in the composer, a chat panel reachable from anywhere, and automations built from a sentence ("When [this happens], then [do this]" in HubSpot) ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows); [Pipedrive AI](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pipedrive-ai)). The shared guardrail is explicit: Shopify Sidekick "doesn't change your store without your approval", you click **Apply**, and AI-drafted Flow automations are inactive by default ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/productivity-tools/sidekick/features); [Craftshift](https://craftshift.com/?p=18881)); Intuit Assist drafts overdue-invoice reminders that you review and edit before sending ([QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/innovation/intuit-assist-for-quickbooks/)); Salesforce's Sales Management agent updates only fields listed for autonomous updates and leaves the rest as suggestions ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/sales-cloud-top-7-salesforce-summer-26-features/)).

**Notable variants.** Entry points split between a side panel opened from a persistent field (Shopify Sidekick, with Shift+click for fullscreen; HubSpot Breeze Assistant), a global shortcut (Notion AI ⇧⌘J / ⇧Ctrl+J), and chat-first home pages (HoneyBook, Wix Aria) ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/productivity-tools/sidekick/features); [Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/everything-you-can-do-with-notion-ai)). Transparency is thin in business-data assistants — Sidekick's only "show your work" is a navigation arrow to the page it used plus thumbs up/down — whereas Intercom Fin cites the help article behind each answer and gives admins answer inspection ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/productivity-tools/sidekick/features); [eesel on Fin](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/fin-guidance)). Attio logs every agent run with tools called, records changed and credits used, and HubSpot's Agent Hub lists prospecting, data, deal-progression and customer agents behind a credit meter ([Releasebot Attio](https://releasebot.io/updates/attio); [HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/use-breeze)). The MCP endpoint is the cheap 2026 way to expose a small tool's data to outside assistants: Pipedrive, HoneyBook and Bonsai all shipped one, Bonsai's dated June 25, 2026 ([Pipedrive updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates); [Bonsai help](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/15519733-bonsai-mcp-server); [HoneyBook AI](https://help.honeybook.com/en/collections/10435807-honeybook-ai)). Pipedrive's email summariser returns a summary, sentiment, a **1–10 readiness-to-buy score** and action items ([Pipedrive AI](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pipedrive-ai)). Dubsado and 17hats show no AI in official sources, and Bonsai's AI story is the MCP server rather than in-app features ([Dubsado](https://dubsado.com/blog/introducing-dubsado-three-point-o); [17hats](https://www.17hats.com/features)). HoneyBook's lead enrichment is on by default ([HoneyBook help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/10777672-about-lead-enrichment-with-honeybook-ai)).

**The numbers.** HubSpot workflows: undo/redo 30 days, action logs 90 days ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows)). Dubsado timing units allow decimals (0.25h = 15 minutes) ([Dubsado help](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/15668762-flow-triggers-general)). Pipedrive auto-deactivates repeatedly failing automations and alerts owners ([Pipedrive updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates)). Intuit's vendor claim for AI reminders: invoices paid "an average of five days faster" ([Insightful Accountant](https://blog.insightfulaccountant.com/new-ai-agents-tackle-tasks-and-boost-productivity)). Vendor guidance on AI panel width: a persistent sidebar takes roughly 20% of the screen (300–400px); spotlight overlays suit single queries in data-dense CRMs ([Composite](https://composite.com/blog/sidebar-beats-spotlight-ai-pattern)).

### Search and keyboard: ⌘K for search-or-command, `/` to focus, single letters to create

**Converged pattern.** Cmd/Ctrl+K opens a combined search and command palette (HubSpot, Attio, Linear), `/` jumps to search (Pipedrive, Attio, Salesforce), single letters create the top objects, J/K move through lists and Esc closes everything. HubSpot's search covers records, activities, pages, workflows, settings, Knowledge Base and Academy, groups results by category, caps at 100 record results, previews on hover with quick actions, and may show a Breeze summary for the top hit ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/account-management/search-within-your-hubspot-account)). Attio offers over 30 quick actions under ⌘K, `n` note, `t` task, `c` compose, a typed prompt plus Tab to start Ask Attio, and `?` for the shortcut list ([Attio help](https://attio.com/help/getting-started/navigating-your-workspace)). Pipedrive maps `1`–`8` to nav tabs, `.` or `+` to quick-add, and L / D / A / P / O / N / R / E to lead, deal, activity, person, organisation, note, product and project ([Pipedrive KB](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/quick-actions-in-pipedrive)). Salesforce Lightning offers `e` to edit, Ctrl+. and Ctrl+Enter, with shortcuts not customisable ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/modules/lightning-experience-productivity/units/elevate-your-daily-productivity)). Slack's Activity view uses ↑/↓, Enter, C to clear and R to mark read ([Slack Help](https://slack.com/help/articles/46751260742035/)); Linear's `h` snoozes ([Linear changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2021-06-17-inbox-snooze-and-easier-issue-merge)).

**Notable variants.** Design commentary recommends building the palette once a product exceeds roughly ten features, then adding single-key shortcuts, with inline shortcut hints, focus trapping in dialogs and no hover-only actions ([SaaSUI.Design](https://saasui.design/blog/7-saas-ui-design-trends-2026); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Mobile expectations are a "needs me" home, business-card scanning and push on assignment (Pipedrive) and fast table views with recorded calls (Attio) ([Pipedrive updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates); [Releasebot Attio](https://releasebot.io/updates/attio)).

**The numbers.** HubSpot: 100 record results maximum. Attio: 30+ quick actions. Pipedrive: 8 numbered nav slots plus 8 create letters.

## Website builders agree on autosave, a pre-flight Publish and a green "Connected" chip

### Editing model: pages panel, section-then-block canvas, global styles

**Converged pattern.** Every leader is visual, on-canvas, section-then-block editing with a left-hand Pages panel whose "+" offers blank versus designed layouts, hover affordances that add a section above or below, and a global Styles panel that cascades fonts and colours site-wide. Squarespace splits Pages into Main navigation and Not linked, adds sections from a categorised library or blank Fluid Engine, and sets fonts via font packs and a five-colour palette generated from an image or primary colour ([Squarespace Pages panel](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/217644727-The-Pages-panel); [Page sections](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360027987711-Page-sections); [Changing colors](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815278-Changing-colors)). Wix Harmony, launched January 2026, puts an action bar above the selected element with an Ask AI button and lets "+ Add → Pages" choose AI-generated pages, templates by category, or blank ([Wix Harmony](https://support.wix.com/en/article/introducing-the-wix-harmony-editor); [Wix Harmony pages](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-harmony-editor-adding-and-managing-pages)). WordPress.com patterns open in "content-only mode, which locks the layout so you can edit text and images safely" ([WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/wordpress-editor/block-pattern/)). Autosave with a separate Publish is dominant: Wix Studio saves "after every change", Webflow autosaves with ⌘S for manual, Framer saves every edit for undo ([Wix Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/studio-editor-saving-previewing-and-publishing-your-site); [Webflow shortcuts](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961359609875-Keyboard-shortcuts-in-Webflow); [Framer](https://www.framer.com/collaborate/)).

**Notable variants.** Squarespace is the outlier and admits it: "Squarespace doesn't have auto-save or revision history features when editing", and undo is lost on Exit ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206543417-Troubleshooting-lost-content); [Undo/redo](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403167416461-Using-undo-and-redo-in-Squarespace)). Shopify edits settings beside a live preview rather than on-canvas, undo applies only to unsaved changes, and Winter '26 lets you click anything and ask Sidekick to make the edit ([Shopify theme editor](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/features-overview); [Shopify Winter '26](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-merchant)). Webflow deprecated its legacy Editor on October 5, 2026 in favour of client seats with Marketer, Content editor and Reviewer roles, and content editors click text directly on the canvas ([Webflow FAQ](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/48412420902675-Legacy-Editor-deprecation-FAQ)). Comments (press **C** in Webflow and Framer), branches, reviews and staging URLs are agency features; the lightweight equivalent is Wix Studio's Get Client Feedback link valid two weeks ([Webflow comments](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961339882131-Comments); [Framer](https://www.framer.com/collaborate/); [Wix Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/studio-editor-saving-previewing-and-publishing-your-site)). Mobile apps edit existing text, images and buttons but not layout; Squarespace's app "cannot add or rearrange blocks" and Webflow and Framer have no mobile editor ([Squarespace app](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002093708-Edit-your-site-with-the-Squarespace-app); [Wix app](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-editor-editing-your-site-on-your-mobile-device)).

**The numbers.** Webflow undo/redo covers up to 100 changes per session ([Webflow updates](https://webflow.com/updates/cms-item-creation-and-undo-redo-updates)). Shopify templates allow up to 25 sections and 1,250 blocks ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/theme-structure/sections-and-blocks)). Squarespace deleted pages are restorable for 30 days ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/217644727-The-Pages-panel)). Squarespace palette: five colours.

### Preview, publish and versions: one button, a post-publish sheet, every publish restorable

**Converged pattern.** A single top-right Publish button; Wix, Webflow and Framer then show a sheet with the live URL and a View Site action, while Squarespace and WordPress.com model publishing as a visibility switch (Private / Password Protected / Public; Coming Soon / Public) and Shopify as removing the password via a Launch store button that appears on Home, the Online Store page and the theme editor ([Wix publishing](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-editor-publishing-your-site); [Framer publishing](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/publishing-your-framer-website/); [Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002088548-Publish-or-hide-your-site); [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/privacy-settings/launch-your-website/); [Shopify launch](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-prepare-for-launch)). Framer's publish sheet doubles as a pre-flight: an "Optimized" label turns red when adjustments are needed and View Changes lists edits with each editor's avatar ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/publishing-your-framer-website/)). Version history is table stakes: Wix Site History lists every saved and published version with date, time and collaborator, filterable and restorable ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/viewing-and-managing-your-site-history)); Webflow creates a restore point every 50th auto-save and saves the current version before any restore ([Webflow backups](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961244069395)); "Clicking 'Publish' creates a new version" in Framer, any of which can be deployed to the custom domain ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/staging-and-versions/)); Shopify keeps the previous theme as a draft so rollback is republishing it ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/using-themes/managing-themes/publishing-themes)).

**Notable variants.** Preview richness peaks at Wix Studio: a device drop-down, free-form width in px, drag handles, a "Preview as Test Site" and a phone QR code ([Wix Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/studio-editor-saving-previewing-and-publishing-your-site)). Shopify's mobile preview "is the only device option" ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/features-overview)). Scheduled whole-site publishing exists only in Wix, via an Automation with a Scheduled time trigger and Publish site action ([Wix Automations](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-automations-scheduling-your-site-to-publish)); Webflow "can't schedule a single-page publish" and Framer needs marketplace plugins ([Webflow](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/50064706725395-Publish-a-single-page); [Framer plugin](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/plugins/publishr/)). Squarespace has no version history at all ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206543417-Troubleshooting-lost-content)). Framer's file-level snapshots run every five minutes for four hours, hourly for 24 hours, then daily ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-can-i-revert-to-a-previous-working-version-of-my-file/)).

**The numbers.** Share-preview link expiry: Wix Studio 2 weeks, Shopify mobile 2 days ([Wix Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/studio-editor-saving-previewing-and-publishing-your-site); [Shopify app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/shopify-app/mobile-online-store)). Webflow restore point cadence: every 50 auto-saves. Wix CMS collections: weekly automatic backup plus up to 3 manual ([Wix CMS](https://support.wix.com/en/article/cms-formerly-content-manager-backing-up-your-collections)).

### Domain and launch: free subdomain, buy-or-connect, a records table, a status chip, automatic SSL

**Converged pattern.** Every product issues a free subdomain at creation (`*.squarespace.com`, `*.wixsite.com`, `*.webflow.io`, `*.framer.app`, `*.myshopify.com`, `*.wordpress.com`), then offers Buy versus Connect versus Transfer. Connect splits into an automatic route ("log in to your domain provider account and we'll take care of the rest" in Wix; Entri Quick connect in Webflow; GoDaddy/IONOS auto-connect in Shopify) and a manual route that is always a Type / Host / Value table of A records plus a `www` CNAME plus a verification TXT or CNAME, ending in a Verify button and a status label ([Wix GoDaddy](https://support.wix.com/en/article/connecting-your-godaddy-domain-to-wix-2745729); [Webflow Quick connect](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961266716307-Quick-connect-a-custom-domain); [Shopify manual](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/domains/add-a-domain/connecting-domains/connect-domain-manual)). Squarespace's manual path is a verification CNAME to `verify.squarespace.com`, `www` CNAME to `ext-cust.squarespace.com` and four A records, with a green **"Connected"** label once linked and a warning to leave MX records alone ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205812378-Connect-a-third-party-domain-to-your-Squarespace-site)). SSL is automatic everywhere: Shopify "automatically creates a new TLS certificate at no cost", Framer "generates and manages your SSL certificate" once DNS resolves, Webflow renews while DNS points at it ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/domains/add-a-domain); [Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/do-i-have-to-setup-ssl-when-adding-a-custom-domain-to-a-framer-project/); [Webflow SSL](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961362849811-SSL-hosting)). Launch checklists are explicit in Squarespace (an 8-step help article from design through promote), Shopify (a General checklist ending in "Remove the online store password" and a Launch store card on Home) and WordPress.com (a Site Setup sidebar, then a single Launch site button gated on a confirmed email address) ([Squarespace checklist](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022518252-Site-launch-checklist); [Shopify checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist); [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/privacy-settings/launch-your-website/)).

**Notable variants.** Wix requires a paid plan before connecting and recommends nameservers over pointing because it "can't troubleshoot another host" ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/pointing-vs-name-servers-domain-connection-methods)); Framer and Webflow allow free publishing to a platform subdomain, with Framer offering free vanity subdomains on `framer.website` that go live "within seconds" ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-to-connect-a-custom-domain/)). Shopify's launch gate is plan, store address and payment method with test mode off ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-prepare-for-launch)). Cloudflare proxying is unsupported across Wix, Webflow, Framer and Shopify (records must be DNS-only) ([Webflow Cloudflare](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961315914515-Connect-your-Cloudflare-domain-to-Webflow)). Email on the domain is handled by pointing to Google Workspace (Squarespace, Wix) or by SPF/DKIM guidance (Shopify) ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022518252-Site-launch-checklist); [Shopify checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist)).

**The numbers.** Propagation quoted at 24–48 hours (Squarespace, Wix, Webflow, Framer, Shopify) and up to 72 hours (WordPress.com) ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205812378-Connect-a-third-party-domain-to-your-Squarespace-site); [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/domains/connect-a-domain-alternative-method/)). Squarespace unlinks an unverified domain after 15 days. Shopify allows 20 domains (1,000 on Plus) and one change of the myshopify name ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/domains/add-a-domain)). Squarespace's Site launch checklist has 8 steps.

Search Console data takes about 72 hours to appear ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205813918-Verifying-your-site-with-Google-Search-Console)); Shopify quotes 48–72 hours to index ([Shopify checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist)). Social image: 1200×630 (Framer).

## Onboarding converges on a short checklist, sample data and an editable brief

**Converged pattern.** The reference anatomy is Shopify's Setup guide: a checklist on Home with a subdued "0 out of 3 steps completed" line, each step a checkbox row with its own chevron, the first step open by default, a "Dismiss Guide" x and a "Toggle setup guide" collapse, a toast ("Step 1 completed!") on completion, and verb-plus-object buttons that deep-link to the task ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/app-home/patterns/compositions/setup-guide.md)). Each checklist drives to one first-value action: Shopify's first product (or "start with a sample product"), Squarespace's publish, WordPress.com's Launch site ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/how-to-start-a-shopify-store/); [SEOSpace](https://www.seospace.co/blog/how-to-publish-a-squarespace-website); [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/support/getting-started-with-wordpress-com/)). CRMs funnel new users into connect your mailbox and calendar, import a spreadsheet with a column-mapping step, and invite teammates; Pipedrive supplies a sample spreadsheet and Attio a six-stage import checklist, while Attio's email sync creates People and Company records with no import at all ([Pipedrive import](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/importing-data-into-pipedrive-with-spreadsheets); [Attio import](https://attio.com/help/reference/imports-exports/import-data-into-attio-via-csv); [Attio sync](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/syncing-people-and-companies)). Website builders now lead with an AI path: three to six conversational questions with "Help me answer" and "Skip question" affordances, a visible, editable site brief or profile (Wix "Edit the site brief", Harmony's Site Profile, WordPress.com's site brief with planned pages), generation with Regenerate / Tweak / Shuffle colours / Shuffle layout controls, then a fork to connect a domain, open the editor, or set up the business ([Wix AI site](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-editor-creating-an-ai-generated-site); [Wix Harmony AI](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-harmony-editor-creating-a-site-with-ai); [WordPress Agent](https://wordpress.com/support/ai-website-builder/)).

**Notable variants.** Payment gates differ: Squarespace keeps trial sites private until a paid plan and publishing is the upgrade trigger; WordPress.com requires checkout before the agent even builds; Shopify requires a plan before the password comes off; Framer and Webflow publish free to a platform subdomain ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205810548-Accessing-and-sharing-a-trial-site); [WordPress Agent](https://wordpress.com/support/ai-website-builder/); [Framer](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/publishing-your-framer-website/)). Squarespace Blueprint is a step-by-step builder (site title, brand personality, homepage, pages, palette, fonts, curated images) rather than a chat, and is computer-only ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206545337-Choosing-the-right-template)). Benchmark data argues against auto-launched linear tours: self-triggered tours double engagement, launcher-driven tours complete at 67% versus 31%, and tours over five steps lose more than half of users ([Chameleon](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report)); action-oriented tours complete at 72% versus 16% for passive showcases ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/website-tooltip/)). Trial-model data is bimodal: credit-card-required trials convert at a 25–35% median versus 4–6% for no-card and reverse trials ([Userpilot citing ChartMogul](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)).

**The numbers.** Checklist completion averages **19.2%** and activation **37.5%** across Userpilot's 2025 dataset, with AI tools at 54.8% and FinTech near 5% ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/saas-product-metrics/); [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/user-onboarding-metrics/)). Median time-to-value is **1 day 12h 23m** overall and **2 days 8h 16m for CRM & Sales**, with a "safe range" under two days ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/time-to-value)). Users who touch a checklist complete about 5 items; progress indicators lift tour completion 12%; modals are dismissed 37.5% of the time, 38% of those within four seconds, and should stay under 26 words ([Chameleon](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report)). Pipedrive import: XLS/XLSX/CSV, one tab, under 50 MB, row limit stated as 50,000 or 200,000 depending on article version ([Pipedrive](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/importing-data-into-pipedrive-with-spreadsheets)). Squarespace trial: 14 days, no card until publish; Shopify: 3 days then $1/month for three months ([SEOSpace](https://www.seospace.co/blog/how-to-publish-a-squarespace-website); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/website/builder)).

## Client-facing moments reduce to one link, forced order and no account

### Lead-to-cash and e-sign: the combined document is the product

**Converged pattern.** HoneyBook, Dubsado, Bonsai, Moxie and 17hats all run the same chain: an embeddable lead form auto-creates a project or lead in an Inquiry stage and notifies the owner; a stage-based pipeline; then a single combined document in which the client picks services, signs and pays from one emailed link, with each step locked until the previous one completes. HoneyBook's smart file bundles services, invoice, contract, scheduling and questionnaire, and "your client will not be able to access the invoice until after they sign the contract"; signing is click the line, type or draw ([HoneyBook lead forms](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/6554485-manage-your-leads-in-honeybook); [HoneyBook contracts](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9773326-how-clients-interact-with-contracts-in-a-smart-file); [HoneyBook combinations](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5949959-commonly-combined-steps-in-smart-files)). Dubsado's proposal is the only form that generates an invoice, with Include contract and Include invoice toggles and a client path of pick package → Contract tab → Invoice tab ([Dubsado](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/6943800-connect-a-contract-and-invoice-to-a-proposal-in-2-0)). 17hats' 3-in-1 document enforces that the quote must be accepted before the contract can be signed or the invoice paid, and editing after acceptance forces re-acceptance ([17hats](https://help.17hats.com/en/articles/924358-combined-documents)). Bonsai shows a payment modal immediately after the client countersigns a contract with a deposit ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/6683807-how-to-set-an-upfront-payment-deposit-on-contracts)). No tool requires an account to sign or pay; Bonsai states it outright, and HoneyBook's only hardening is an optional emailed numeric code required at the moment of signing or paying ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/1202136-how-clients-pay-invoices-sign-contracts-and-accept-proposals); [HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9768365-how-clients-access-and-submit-smart-files)). The DocuSign reference flow is email → Review Document → consent checkbox → Start tag → Adopt and Sign (choose a style or draw) → Finish ([Fleming College guide](https://tdx.flemingcollege.ca/TDClient/178/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=9767)).

**Notable variants.** Retainers are modelled two ways: Bonsai's "subscription invoice" charges the saved card each cycle, while its "recurring invoice" duplicates a fresh invoice the client pays each time; HoneyBook's recurring invoice behaves like the subscription and clients cannot pause or cancel it themselves ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/1065297-understanding-the-difference-between-subscription-and-recurring-invoices); [HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9586061-client-experience-with-invoices)). HoneyBook adds a Redirect URL after submission, SMS reminders with opt-in, late fees, and requires the client to accept any edited payment schedule when autopay is on ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9768365-how-clients-access-and-submit-smart-files); [HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2209106-editing-a-payment-schedule)). Dubsado's contract-signed trigger fires on the client's signature alone, and "form not completed" reminders reset when a form is resent ([Dubsado](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/15668761-flow-triggers-client-progress)). Approval-only tools let approvers act from the email without logging in, but where legal acceptance matters tools require a typed or drawn signature block rather than a bare button ([ApproveThis](https://www.g2.com/products/approvethis/reviews); [GoProposal](https://help.goproposal.com/en/articles/4134937-how-prospects-and-clients-sign-their-engagement-letter)). Basecamp and Productive have no proposal-contract-invoice flow at all ([Productive](https://productive.io/blog/project-management-software-with-a-client-portal/)).

**The numbers.** DocuSign claims about 80% of agreements complete within a day and 44–50% within 15 minutes, with WhatsApp delivery signed roughly seven times faster than email ([DocuSign](https://www.docusign.com/blog/top-5-e-signature-features-to-get-agreements-signed-faster); [Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/the-wire/docusign-launches-whatsapp-integration-to-accelerate-business-around-the-globe/)). HoneyBook sends at most one payment reminder per day and auto-pays within 24 hours of a due date ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2209077-send-manual-or-automatic-payment-reminders-in-honeybook); [HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5790758-recurring-invoices-in-honeybook)). 17hats Recurring Billing is a $10/month add-on and its processors are Stripe and Square only ([17hats pricing](https://www.17hats.com/pricing)).

### Client portal: per-project link, no password, a visible "not visible to clients" line

**Converged pattern.** Portals split into project-scoped, no-password access (HoneyBook, Bonsai, 17hats, Dubsado) and account-based access (Basecamp, Productive). HoneyBook's project-specific links drop the client straight in; the generic link offers a magic link, password, or a **6-digit emailed code**; tabs are Activity, Files, Payments, Meetings, Notes and Project details, and clients can Attach files but cannot see tasks, automations, the pipeline or anything above the "Not visible to clients" divider ([HoneyBook access](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/11095702-how-clients-access-the-honeybook-client-portal); [HoneyBook portal](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/6428603-your-clients-experience-with-the-client-portal)). Dubsado's portal is one per client across all projects, starts deactivated, logs in by email from a client-specific link and by password from the public link ([Dubsado](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/15920587-what-are-client-portals)). Bonsai's "secure, unique link" portal exposes projects, tasks with feedback, invoices, files and a per-project Messages thread the client can answer from the portal or by email ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/4409019-how-to-use-the-client-portal)). 17hats auto-creates a portal per contact with a tokenised email link and optional PIN ([17hats](https://help.17hats.com/en/articles/1131267-client-portal-overview)). Basecamp's rule is "Everything starts private" with a per-item flag at posting time ([Basecamp](https://5.basecamp-help.com/article/1082-what-clients-can-see-and-do)). Every tool brands the portal with logo, colours and banner, and most support a custom domain via CNAME ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/11655867-brand-the-client-portal-with-your-company-s-look); [Moxie](http://help.withmoxie.com/en/collections/19706142-client-portal)).

**Notable variants.** Moxie sends **no automatic email** when portal access is enabled, which is a documented friction to avoid ([Moxie](https://help.withmoxie.com/en/articles/8238276-client-portal-client-access-and-login)). Productive gives clients a real account with a free seat, a Permission Builder based on Client Collaborator or Client Lead, a "Try Out" preview, and hides worked time from all clients ([Productive](https://help.productive.io/en/articles/2179600-what-can-a-client-see-after-joining-productive); [Productive](https://help.productive.io/en/articles/9273131-customizing-client-permissions)). Formal "approve this deliverable" buttons are rare; Bonsai offers task feedback, Basecamp comments, the rest rely on e-signature. Bonsai clients cannot view or manage saved cards in the portal ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/13179357-how-to-save-a-client-card-on-file-and-use-it-to-pay-invoices)); 17hats clients cannot upload files and see only the 100 most recent documents ([17hats](https://help.17hats.com/en/articles/1131267-client-portal-overview)). Magic-link guidance: single-use, 5–15 minute expiry, a code fallback for mobile, and a clear "send a new link" state ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/a-guide-to-magic-links)).

**The numbers.** Code length: 6 digits (HoneyBook). Portal visibility toggles in Bonsai: Tasks, Project progress, Billing, Team members, with three task-sharing levels ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/10003632-sharing-and-managing-tasks-through-your-client-portal)). Plan gating: portal included on all HoneyBook plans; Bonsai portal from Essentials, client messaging and de-branding from Premium; Moxie white-label and custom domain on Pro at $25/month ([HoneyBook pricing](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing); [Bonsai pricing](https://www.hellobonsai.com/pricing); [Moxie pricing](https://www.withmoxie.com/pricing)).

### Payment UX: Stripe's hosted page is the floor; four statuses; autopay is always an explicit checkbox

**Converged pattern.** Stripe's Hosted Invoice Page defines the floor: a long random private URL showing details, amount and status, every enabled payment method with Link autofill, a Pay button, PDF download of invoice and receipt, SCA in a modal, and an expiry page that never dead-ends — expired links redirect to a page with the business's contact details or a recovery form ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/hosted-invoice-page)). HoneyBook's in-document invoice page lists items and the payment schedule, "Clients can't get past the payment page without paying", offers card, bank transfer or Affirm, an optional tip and promo code, an Auto-Pay checkbox, and a client-chosen Split payment versus Pay in full with later installments due monthly on the saved card ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9586061-client-experience-with-invoices)). Every payment triggers a confirmation email with a receipt link, and the owner sees it in an Activity feed and the Paid / Processing / Upcoming / Overdue widget ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5339081-about-your-honeybook-homepage)). Nobody silently stores cards: HoneyBook uses a checkbox at first payment, Bonsai an emailed card-capture link ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/1065297-understanding-the-difference-between-subscription-and-recurring-invoices)).

**Notable variants.** Stripe's saved-method default for accounts created after May 30, 2025 is "Offer" (a consent checkbox), with partial payments and local-currency Adaptive Pricing available ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/hosted-invoice-page)). Dubsado and Moxie route through connected Stripe, Square or PayPal rather than embedding card entry ([Dubsado](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/6943800-connect-a-contract-and-invoice-to-a-proposal-in-2-0); [Moxie](https://www.withmoxie.com/product)). HoneyBook's tips can be requested on every payment or only the final one, and since February 2026 a final-installment tip is computed on the full project total ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/6680170-enable-tips-and-automatic-tip-reminders-in-honeybook); [HoneyBook updates](https://www.honeybook.com/blog/product-updates-february-2026)). Bonsai's declined subscription card notifies both parties and leaves the invoice outstanding ([Bonsai](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/2715822-what-to-do-when-a-client-s-payment-method-fails-in-a-subscription-invoice-series)). The loudest negative review theme across HoneyBook and Bonsai is funds held and high fees, which a Stripe-direct model on the agency's own account sidesteps ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/honeybook-alternatives); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/238825/Bonsai/reviews/)).

**The numbers.** Stripe: **87% of invoices paid within 24 hours**, Link checkouts about 3x faster with a 14% lift in returning-user conversion ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/invoices); [Stripe Link](https://stripe.com/en-mx/payments/onelink)); invoice URLs expire 30 days after the due date and never later than 120 days; receipt links expire within 30 days ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/hosted-invoice-page); [Stripe receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)). HoneyBook: bank transfers take 7–8 days, ACH 1.5%, cards quoted inconsistently as 2.7% + 10¢ or 2.9% + 25¢ ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2209043-payment-processing-times-transaction-fees-and-client-payment-options); [HoneyBook pricing](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing)). Clicks to pay: open link → optional email code → sign → card → Pay for a new client, two clicks with a saved method for a returning one.

## Notifications, help and billing borrow their shapes from Slack, Linear, Intercom and Stripe

### Notifications: one feed, filter tabs, "mark read" versus "clear", a priority tier

**Converged pattern.** Slack's Activity view (rolling out from January 2026) and Linear's Inbox both consolidate every notification into one feed with filter tabs, bulk clear, and a semantic split between "Mark as read" (unbold, keep) and "Clear" (hide into a recoverable Cleared filter); Slack's DM badge now counts unread conversations, not messages, and offers Detailed versus Dense layouts ([Slack Help](https://slack.com/help/articles/46751260742035/)). Linear's September 3, 2026 Priority inbox separates notifications needing attention from an "Other" tab, with product-chosen defaults the user can override, and snooze (`h`) auto-unsnoozes on new activity ([Linear changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-09-03-priority-inbox); [Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/snooze)). Among the small-business tools, HoneyBook keeps the bell for payments and signatures and routes client email to the Inbox ([HoneyBook](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2459619-about-the-notifications-center-in-honeybook)); Attio's inbox holds mentions, shared emails and assigned tasks ([Attio](https://attio.com/help/getting-started/navigating-your-workspace)); Squarespace keeps a bell "for time-sensitive site alerts" and the Squarespace app pushes a weekly Saturday analytics insight ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/212260078-Your-site-s-main-menu); [Squarespace app](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002093708-Edit-your-site-with-the-Squarespace-app)).

**Notable variants.** Linear's Inbox is not customisable, but each notification type can be routed to email, push or Slack separately ([Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/inbox)). Digest guidance from notification-infrastructure vendors: batch by a grouping key, keep time-critical items out of digests, deliver on a predictable cadence, cap at a few highlights and one chart, and let users set quiet hours ([SuprSend](https://docs.suprsend.com/docs/best-practises-for-batching-digest); [Workclaw](https://www.workclaw.com/blog/notification-styles-compared-nudges-digests-blocks)). Slack's September 2025 stuck-badge regression was rolled back within days, a reminder that unread state is the most visible thing to break ([Slack status](https://slack-status.com/2025-09-15)).

**The numbers.** Slack default tabs shown: All and DMs only. Linear tiers: Priority and Other. Keyboard: C clear, R mark read, `h` snooze.

### Help: search first, AI answer with citation, then an explicit human hand-off

**Converged pattern.** Intercom's Messenger moved article search to the top so customers search or browse before being offered a chat, and the October 31, 2025 Fin Messenger redesign added a "clearer indication when Fin hands over a conversation to a human teammate" ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/2407-now-more-customers-will-help-themselves-with-article-search-in-the-intercom-messenger); [Intercom changes](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/page/35)). Fin cites the article behind each answer, gives admins answer inspection, escalation rules and off-limits topics ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/fin-guidance)). HubSpot's Breeze Assistant grounds answers in HubSpot Academy, the knowledge base and your own CRM data, acting as both help and data assistant ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/hubspot-breeze-assistant)). "What's new" is a public dated page: Notion's releases page gives each entry a headline, a short description and a "Try it" link; Linear's changelog mixes a headline feature with fix bullets ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/releases/page/4); [Linear](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-09-03-priority-inbox)).

**Notable variants.** Attio's `?` opens help and the shortcut list from anywhere ([Attio](https://attio.com/help/getting-started/navigating-your-workspace)). Release announcements are the leading in-app modal use case (~40% of modals), and hover- or custom-triggered modals complete at 51% versus 39% on page load ([Chameleon](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report)). The resolution metric is contested: a community thread argues Fin is credited whenever a human replies after it, with one user reporting about 30% confirmed resolution ([Intercom community](https://community.intercom.com/fin-product-feedback-member-group-49/fin-s-flawed-resolution-assumption-10516)).

**The numbers.** Fin resolution: 54% on Intercom's page, 67% in a late-2025 roundup, ~30% customer-confirmed in one report; priced at $0.99 per resolution ([Intercom](https://intercom.com:443/fin); [createwith](https://createwith.com/tool/intercom/updates/intercom-ships-200-updates-in-2025-fin-3-reaches-67-average-resolution-rate); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-per-resolution-2025)). In-app microsurveys complete at ~15% with three answer buttons optimal; CSAT 20%, NPS 9% ([Chameleon](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report)).

### Billing self-service: Stripe's Customer Portal with deep links, no hidden cancel path

**Converged pattern.** Stripe's Customer Portal is the reference: one hosted, brandable page where the customer updates billing details and tax IDs, changes payment method, switches among up to 10 plans with proration, cancels immediately or at period end with an optional reason survey and retention coupon, and pays or downloads current and past invoices ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management)). Deep-link flow types (`subscription_cancel`, `subscription_update`, `payment_method_update`) send the customer straight to the task from the product's own account page ([Stripe API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/customer_portal/configuration)). Receipts are itemised and automatic once "Successful payments" emails are enabled ([Stripe receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)).

**Notable variants.** Trial end is tied to a value moment rather than a date in Squarespace (publishing is the upgrade) and to launch in Shopify ([SEOSpace](https://www.seospace.co/blog/how-to-publish-a-squarespace-website); [Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-prepare-for-launch)). The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025 on procedural grounds, but ROSCA, FTC Section 5 and auto-renewal laws in more than half of U.S. states still require a simple cancellation path ([Gunderson](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)). Among the client tools, pricing is now HoneyBook $29/$49/$109 billed yearly, Dubsado $335/$525 per year, Bonsai $9–$49 per user per month annual, Moxie $10/$20/$32 annual, 17hats a single $60/month with paid add-ons, Productive $10/$25 per user with free client seats ([HoneyBook pricing](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing); [Dubsado pricing](https://www.dubsado.com/pricing); [Bonsai pricing](https://www.hellobonsai.com/pricing); [Moxie pricing](https://www.withmoxie.com/pricing); [17hats pricing](https://www.17hats.com/pricing); [Productive](https://productive.io/blog/project-management-software-with-a-client-portal/)).

**The numbers.** Portal sessions expire after 5 minutes unused, then within an hour of last activity; 10 products maximum for plan switching; 40+ languages; cannot be iframed ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management)). Free-trial conversion: median 8%, bimodal with 20% of products below 2.5% and 23% above 25% ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)). Trial lengths: Dubsado 21 days no card, Moxie 14, Bonsai and 17hats 7 ([Dubsado pricing](https://www.dubsado.com/pricing); [Moxie pricing](https://www.withmoxie.com/pricing); [Bonsai pricing](https://www.hellobonsai.com/pricing); [17hats pricing](https://www.17hats.com/pricing)).

## The visual language is light-first, one-accent, 14px on an 8px grid

### Typography: one neutral grotesque, 13–14px base, tabular numbers for money

**Converged pattern.** Every system in the set uses a single neutral sans (Inter at Shopify and Linear, Geist at Vercel, Atlassian Sans, SF Pro) at a 13–14px UI base, a roughly 1.2 modular scale rounded to a 4px grid, Regular for body, Medium for UI, Bold or Semibold for headings and metrics, and explicit tabular numerals for money. Polaris v10 is a 1.2 ratio from a 14px base rounded to multiples of 4, giving 12/14/16/20/24/28/32/40 ([Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/whats-new/version-10-typography)); Atlassian's body is 14/20 with headings 32/36 down to 12/16 and dedicated Bold "Metric" styles at 28, 24 and 16px ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/typography)); Geist names styles by pixel size with 14 as the default button and 13 as the label, and "Tabular is used with `text-label-13` when conveying numbers" ([Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/typography)). Linear's redesign uses Inter Display for headings and Inter for everything else ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Apple says "avoid light font weights" and "minimize the number of typefaces" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)).

**Notable variants.** Linear and Geist sit at 13px, which third-party teardowns tie to power-user density; Polaris and Atlassian at 14px with 16px for long-form are the better fit for non-technical owners ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Stripe's brand face Söhne is a paid licence with reconstructed negative tracking at display sizes, and one source claims the Dashboard uses a system stack instead ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=6754); [opendesigner](https://opendesigner.io/design-systems/stripe)). No product UI in the set uses a serif for display; Apple's New York is offered as an option only ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)).

**The numbers.** Minimums: 13px for interactive text and 12px captions (Shopify); 11pt iOS, 10pt macOS (Apple) ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/visual-design); [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Atlassian paragraph spacing 16/12/8; iOS Body 17/22, macOS Body 13/16 ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/typography); [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)).

### Layout and density: 8px rhythm, flat bordered surfaces, 3–5 bottom tabs on mobile

**Converged pattern.** Atlassian's scale is the canonical one: base 8 with 0/2/4/6/8/12/16/20/24/32/40/48/64/80, small steps for icon-text gaps and cell padding, medium for button padding and cards, large between page sections ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/spacing)). Calm comes from flat, border-delimited surfaces with small lightness steps rather than shadows: Geist's Background 1 and 2 with floating materials reserved for menus and modals, and Linear's "depth comes from these background shifts, not drop shadows" ([Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/materials); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Stripe Apps "default to ContextView" (a side drawer) and use the blocking FocusView only for start-to-finish tasks, with action buttons in the header so they survive scrolling ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/design); [Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/action-buttons)). Mobile: a navigation bar for 3–5 destinations, a drawer beyond five ([Android](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns?hl=en); [SAP Fiori](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-android/v25-8/components/m3-standard-components/navigation-bar/usage)).

**Notable variants.** Linear's reconstructed density is list rows of 32–36px with 6px block and 12px inline padding, which the author says "suits power-user apps, not first-time marketing pages" ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Settings guidance is to show about five common options and put the rest behind Advanced ([SaaSUI.Design](https://saasui.design/blog/7-saas-ui-design-trends-2026)). Shopify's theme editor shows a double sidebar at or above 1600px and stacks below ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/features-overview)). No primary source publishes sidebar widths or content max-widths for Linear, Notion, Stripe or Vercel.

**The numbers.** Radii from Geist: 6px tooltip, 12px menu and modal, 16px fullscreen ([Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/materials)). Grid: 4px base, 8px rhythm. Mobile tabs: 3–5.

### Colour: a 10-step neutral ramp, one accent, semantic hues never alone, light first

**Converged pattern.** Geist's ten scales each run 100–1000 with a fixed role per step: 100 default background, 200 hover, 300 active, 400–600 borders, 700–800 high-contrast backgrounds, 900 secondary text, 1000 primary text, plus "draw each border once" and translucent shadows ([Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)). One brand accent is reserved for primary action, focus ring and selection (Atlassian's Brand role; Linear's reconstructed "Magic Blue" used only on the mark, focus ring and primary button) ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)). Semantic colours carry fixed meanings and are never the only signal: Shopify sets green for positive or completed, yellow for paused or non-urgent attention, orange as "the strongest non-blocking color" for needs-attention, red for errors only and "not for drawing attention" ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/visual-design)); Atlassian's roles are Neutral, Brand, Information, Success, Warning, Danger, Discovery and Accent, with selected states paired with a checkmark, underline or border ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)).

**Notable variants.** Linear generates themes in LCH from three inputs (base, accent, contrast), replacing 98 hand-set variables, and the contrast dial can produce very high-contrast themes ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Dark mode is default-ish only at Linear; Atlassian supports exactly two token themes, and Salesforce's SLDS 2 Cosmos theme went GA in Winter '26 with dark mode in beta and background images removed for accessibility ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color); [Salesforce Dictionary](https://salesforcedictionary.com/blogs/salesforce-slds-2-cosmos-theme-2026)). Stripe Apps deliberately restrict element colours "because color contrast is an important aspect of accessible UI" ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/design)). A 2025 eye-tracking study found no eye-strain difference between modes and better reading acuity in light mode ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)).

**The numbers.** 10 neutral steps; 9 saturated hues at Atlassian; contrast 4.5:1 for text under 24px, 3:1 for larger text and essential UI ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)). Linear's faintest text tone reconstructs at about 3.6:1, suitable only for timestamps ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)).

### Components: one primary per view, Stripe's empty-state formula, drawers before modals

**Converged pattern.** One emphasised primary action per view, placed in the header; neutral secondary buttons; a text tertiary ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/action-buttons); [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color); [SaaSUI.Design](https://saasui.design/blog/7-saas-ui-design-trends-2026)). Stripe's empty-state rule is the most precise published: the title states what is missing as a short phrase with a period ("No successful payments."), the description is under 14 words in active voice, the action mirrors the title ("No customers" → "Add customer", never "Get started"), filtered-empty states show "Clear filters" rather than a create button, and the render order is loading → error → empty → content ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/empty-state)). Loading: spinners suit single modules and waits of roughly 2–10 seconds; beyond 10 seconds use a progress bar with an estimate; skeletons only where structure is predictable ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/); [PatternFly](https://patternfly.org/components/skeleton/design-guidelines)). Toasts confirm in noun + verb form ("Product updated") ([Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/deprecated/toast)). Dialogs versus sheets: default to a side drawer, block only for deep or uninterruptible work ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/design)).

**Notable variants.** Atlassian defines Metric text styles for KPI tiles; Stripe documents a Chart layout pattern but no system publishes tile sizes or sparkline rules ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/typography); [Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns)). Shopify warns that partial icon use "looks broken" and asks for icons paired with text ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/visual-design)). Stripe's section-level empty state is compact: large padding, a 1px dashed border, body-size unbolded title, caption-size description ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/empty-state)). No primary source specifies Kanban or inline-editing rules for any of these systems.

**The numbers.** Empty-state description ≤14 words; toast ≈3 words; spinner ceiling 10 seconds; modal copy ≤26 words ([Stripe Apps](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/empty-state); [Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/deprecated/toast); [NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/); [Chameleon](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report)).

### Motion, icons, copy and accessibility: Atlassian's ladder, 2px strokes, sentence case, 3:1 focus rings

**Converged pattern.** Atlassian publishes the only complete motion token set in the group: instant 0ms for focus, 50ms list hover, 100ms pressed and popup exit, 150ms popup enter, 200ms modal and flag exit, 250ms modal and flag enter, 400ms page-level, 600ms onboarding; exits are 50–100ms shorter than entrances; only transform and opacity animate; modals scale 95→100%; and "don't delay errors, confirmations, or focus indicators behind an animation" ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion/applying-motion)). Icons are 2px strokes on a 24px grid (Lucide's default, with `absoluteStrokeWidth` keeping 2px at any size) and weight-matched to text (SF Symbols) ([Lucide](https://lucide.dev/guide/basics/stroke-width); [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Copy is sentence case, status labels are one to three words, confirmations are noun + verb with no article and no period, plain language is justified because merchants "have varying levels of literacy", and "needs attention" is orange plus text, never colour alone ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/content); [shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/visual-design)). Accessibility targets WCAG AA — 4.5:1 for small text, 3:1 for large text, essential UI and focus rings — and Atlassian added an offset focus border specifically "so the focus border reaches the required 3:1 contrast" against button fills ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color); [Atlassian community](https://community.developer.atlassian.com/t/psa-button-focus-state-changes-are-coming/67910)).

**Notable variants.** Published principle sets reduce to clarity and restraint: SLDS "Clarity, Efficiency, Consistency, Beauty", HubSpot Canvas "Clear, Human, Inbound, Integrated, Collaborative", Geist "simplicity, minimalism, and speed" ([SLDS](https://spring-20.lightningdesignsystem.com/guidelines/overview); [Canvas](https://canvas.hubspot.com/); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/font)). 2026 commentary calls the dark dense "Linear look" generic, pushes calm design and progressive disclosure, and says "products that still badge every AI touchpoint now look dated"; bento grids and the Liquid-Glass-flavoured translucency belong on marketing pages, and glass "struggles with long body copy" ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026); [SaaSUI.Design](https://saasui.design/blog/7-saas-ui-design-trends-2026); [Line25](https://line25.com/articles/web-design-trends-2026/)). Salesforce's Path can fire a confetti celebration on Closed Won, and celebration animations are listed as a 2026 pattern ([salesforcegeek](https://salesforcegeek.in/path-setting-in-salesforce-with-example/); [SaaSUI.Design](https://saasui.design/blog/7-saas-ui-design-trends-2026)). No fetched system documents `prefers-reduced-motion`, and no fetched guideline endorses emoji in UI chrome.

**The numbers.** Motion 50–600ms, nothing above 400ms outside onboarding; focus duration 0ms; easing `cubic-bezier(0.4, 1, 0.6, 1)` for interactions ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion/applying-motion)). Icon stroke 2px. Status labels 1–3 words. Contrast 4.5:1 / 3:1. A 2px ring with 2px offset via `:focus-visible` is the common tutorial pattern, not an official spec ([devpath](https://www.devpath.com/courses/css-design-patterns/accessible-radio-button-focus)).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is not any single pattern but how little the categories differ once you look at the client-facing and owner-facing moments side by side: a CRM record page, a builder's publish sheet and a portal's invoice page all converge on the same shape — status at the top, one primary action, a link that never dead-ends, and a human click before anything irreversible happens. That convergence means a small product does not need to invent an interaction language; it needs to adopt the published one (Atlassian's spacing and motion, Geist's colour roles, Stripe's empty-state and hosted-page rules, Shopify's checklist anatomy, HoneyBook's four payment buckets, Linear's two-tier inbox) and spend its originality elsewhere. The benchmark numbers are soft — almost all vendor-reported, several contested — so they work as floors to beat rather than targets to hit, and the honest internal metrics (confirmed help resolution, same-session first client send, invoices paid within a day on the agency's own Stripe account) will matter more than the industry averages.

The openings are specific. None of the client-portal tools offers a first-class "Approve / Request changes" on a deliverable; none of the builders except Wix schedules a whole-site publish; Squarespace still has no autosave or version history; Moxie enables portals silently; HoneyBook under-serves internal team tasks; and business-data AI assistants almost universally skip the citation step that support bots now take for granted. A product that combines the converged patterns with those five or six gaps — approval as a document-grade moment, every publish a restorable version, a portal invitation that always sends, AI that cites the client record it used — would be recognisably in the same family as the leaders while exceeding them where a non-technical owner feels it most.
