# The website builder's look: three bold directions. November 9, 2026

**For:** Eric, to pick a direction before the open builder items (34, 35, 36 and 118 to 125) get built. Nothing in the editor changes until you pick.
**Figma:** [Website builder, bold directions](https://www.figma.com/design/HSP7ScFSz2mg1xGzHRrEZi?node-id=64-2), a new page in the same file as the sales pages. Three sections, A, B and C, each with a desktop frame and a phone frame. A also has a dark frame.
**No client data.** Every drawing shows a made-up bakery, Marlow's Kitchen, which is the name the browser tests already use. The screenshots of today's editor came from the test harness with made-up content, not from a real site.

## What I walked

The editor as it is on main, in the browser tests' harness (the real page, fake data), on a 1440 by 900 desktop, in dark mode, and on an iPhone 13 in Safari: the Website overview, Edit mode and its canvas, the section bar on a selected section, the section editor, Add section (Blocks, Files and Content tree tabs), Pages, Styles, the Page menu, the photo picker and its Stock tab, the phone frame on the canvas, and Publish.

## What feels dated or heavy today

1. **One grey strip of about eighteen controls.** Exit, three page tabs with a dotted add-page stub, Page, Undo, Redo, "Everything saved", Edit and Comments, three device buttons, Add section, Pages, Styles, Preview, Publish. On the phone the strip scrolls sideways and clips: "Home" becomes "H" and "Page" is cut in half.
2. **Square grey boxes and black.** Every button is a grey-bordered box; the only strong colour is the black Publish button. The rest of Studio OS now uses the plum gradient, pill buttons, floating white cards and Fraunces titles. Open the editor from the Sites page and it looks like a different product.
3. **The section bar is a black box of seven words**: Move, Edit, Duplicate, Copy, Cut, Hide, Delete. On the phone it wraps to two rows and covers the section above the one you picked.
4. **The panels are flat grey slide-overs of stacked cards.** Styles starts with a "Logo and brand colour" card, then "Colours and fonts" with "Ready-made styles"; the section editor shows a Save for reuse row, eight formatting chips, two "Write with AI" chips and two lines of helper text before you reach the words. Lots of reading for a small change.
5. **Pages leaves the editor.** The Pages button in the bar goes to the Pages page in the back office, so changing the menu means leaving Edit mode and coming back. The Pages page itself is good.
6. **Publish is a separate white page.** The site disappears and "Here's what changes" takes over. The words are right; the trip feels like leaving the room.
7. **The photo picker** is a centred dialog with two-column tiles, the name written over the photo, and a black outline around the active tab that looks like a focus ring that stuck.
8. **On the phone, panels take most of the screen** with a sliver of site at the top, "Collapse" with a left arrow on a bottom sheet, and a floating "+ Add section" button that sits over the words.
9. **Icons are a mix.** Undo, Redo and the devices are icons; everything else is a word. There is no one icon set in the editor, which is item 34.
10. **Dark mode** turns the strip dark and the Publish button light grey. It works but it is the same flat strip.

**What is already right and should stay:** full-screen Edit mode with one bar; everything saves itself with one "saved" word; tap to select on touch screens; the idea that the site is the only big colour on screen; Styles changing the canvas instantly; the plain-English publish list; the Pages page with drag to reorder.

## What Squarespace and Wix do that ours does not

From the November 5 and 8 walks in this folder, plus the editors themselves.

- **Squarespace** has one bar of about six things (Save, Exit, undo, redo, page, device). Hover a section and one row of icons appears; Add section sits between sections. Styles is one panel (Themes, Fonts, Colours, Buttons) that changes the page live. Pages is one panel with nesting and a gear per page. The phone view is the same editor in a phone frame. The header is edited on the canvas.
- **Wix** has Quick Edit: pick a section, and a panel beside the canvas lists only that section's fields, with Content and Layout tabs. Add Section is a left panel with purpose groups and real pictures. A slim vertical toolbar sits on the section's right edge, off the words. You can zoom out to half size to rearrange a long page. Site Design is four rows.
- **Ours already has the content model** (41 section types, pages, styles, versions, comments, scheduled publish). The gap is chrome: how much of it there is, where it sits, and how many steps a small change takes.

## The three directions

The same bakery site is being edited in each, with its Our story section selected, three changes waiting, and the site live.

### A. Floating studio

The page fills the whole screen and every tool floats over it as a white card. A pill toolbar across the top (Exit, the page with a count of pages, Undo, Redo, "Everything saved", the device switch, a Live chip, Preview, the plum Publish). A dock of seven icons on the left (Pages, Add, Styles, Photos, Notes, Versions, Find) replaces three bar buttons and the Page menu. Picking a section shows a small pill of icons on the section and opens one inspector card on the right with Content, Style, Layout and When tabs, the fields, Write with AI, layout pictures and background swatches from the site's own colours. "Add section" is a plum pill on the line between sections. On the phone the toolbar floats at the top, the dock floats at the bottom, and the inspector is a half sheet. Dark mode is drawn: the cards go dark, the site stays the site.

### B. Rail and inspector

The layout Squarespace and Wix users know, in our look. A solid top bar: Exit, the site's name with a Live chip, the pages as tabs in the middle (Home carries its change count, plus an add button), Undo, Redo, Saved, the device switch, Preview and Publish on the right. A slim rail of seven icons with labels down the left. One wide panel next to it that changes with the icon you pick; it is drawn open on Pages, with drag handles, a status chip per page, a gear per page, a drop zone for pages not in the menu, and rows for the header and footer, the not found page and search appearance. The site sits in a rounded frame with a shadow. The selected section gets a vertical toolbar on its right edge (up, down, edit, duplicate, style, remove, more) so nothing covers the words. On the phone the rail becomes a bottom bar and the panel a sheet.

### C. Canvas first

The least chrome. A thin dark bar (Exit, the site's name, Live, the page name in the middle, undo and redo arrows, Saved, devices, Preview, Publish, a more button). Almost everything else happens on the page: tap a heading and a small text toolbar appears above it (Heading, B, I, Link, Write with AI); the section gets a handle on its left edge for grip, up and down, and three small icons on its right for duplicate, remove and more; a bare plum plus sits on the line between sections. The pages open from the page name as a menu, with Add a page, Page settings, Header and footer and Versions underneath. Photos, Site styles and Add section are three round buttons in the bottom corner. The phone is the same, with the handle folded into the right-hand tools.

### How many steps a change takes

Change a heading: today two to three (select, Edit, find the field). A: two (select, type in the card, or double tap the words). B: two. C: one (tap the words).
Reorder a section: today two (select, Move) or a drag. A and B: one click on an arrow, or a drag. C: the same, from the edge handle.
Change the menu: today three (Pages, leave the editor, come back). A: one (Pages in the dock opens a card). B: one (Pages in the rail). C: one (the page name).
Change a colour: today two (Styles, scroll past the logo card). A, B and C: one or two, with the result on the page as you pick.

## Which I'd pick, and why

**A, the floating studio.**

- It is the Fresh & bold look itself: the floating white card, the pill toolbar, the plum Publish, the same Button, Chip and icon parts the sales pages use. The Sites page opens into an editor that looks like the same product.
- It keeps the one rule today's editor gets right, the site is the only big colour, and goes further: the page runs edge to edge and the chrome sits on top of it instead of boxing it in.
- The dock turns four bar buttons and a menu into one place, so the bar gets short and the phone stops scrolling sideways.
- Dark mode is one switch of the colour set; it is already drawn.
- It borrows the best of the other two without changing shape: B's Pages panel is what the dock's Pages card should contain, and C's text toolbar and bare plus between sections work inside A as they are.

Where it needs care: floating cards cover part of the page, so the inspector should be easy to tuck away and the canvas should scroll behind it; it needs checking at 1280 wide, where the toolbar and the inspector come close; and the dock's seven icons need their labels, which are drawn in.

B is the safe pick if you would rather clients see a layout they know from Squarespace. C is the boldest and the fastest to use, but it hides the most, and a first-time client has to discover that words are tappable.

## What this changes on the list

- **34, one icon set and one main button per panel:** becomes the dock, the section pill and the inspector header, in the chosen direction.
- **35, Publish blockers as a checklist:** the publish list opens as a card from the Publish button rather than a separate page, with the blockers as a checklist in it. The words stay.
- **36, hard-coded colours and inline styles:** the editor chrome moves onto the shell's tokens, which is also what makes dark mode fall out.
- **118 to 125** get built in the chosen look, not before it: the colour tags on the canvas, the font list, the page switches, the not found page, the phone bar, the header editing bar, the Search to-do list and the Versions words.
