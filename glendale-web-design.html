The Glendale problem

Being good isn’t the hard part. Being found first is.

The businesses I meet in Glendale are rarely bad at what they do. They’re usually excellent, established, and quietly losing customers to a newer competitor with a faster site and a better-kept Google listing. That gap has almost nothing to do with quality and almost everything to do with what happens in the ninety seconds before someone walks in.

So I start there: what somebody types, what they see, what makes them choose. Then the site gets built around that instead of around a template.

Glendale also rewards businesses that speak to their actual neighborhood. If a meaningful share of your customers search in a language other than English, that is a real strategy decision with real traffic attached, and it is worth talking through rather than ignoring.

Questions I actually get asked

Straight answers.

Are you actually near Glendale?

I'm in Burbank, about ten minutes away. Close enough that meeting in person is normal rather than a special occasion.

My competitor outranks me and their site looks worse. How?

Usually one of three things: their Google Business Profile is better maintained, their site loads faster on a phone, or their pages actually name what they do and where they do it while yours are vague. All three are fixable, and none of them require your site to be prettier than theirs.

Do I need a new website, or just better SEO?

Genuinely depends, and I'll tell you which on the first call. Sometimes the site is solid and the visibility work is a much smaller job. I'd rather sell you the smaller job and keep you than oversell you once.
